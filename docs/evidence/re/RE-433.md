## Stage weights and the appearance actor

`MPGroundData.item_weights` (header + 0x84) points to 20 bytes in the
stage's own map file, one weight per common `ITKind`. Bonus stages store
NULL. The ROM test checks Castle's and Zebes's US tables
(`relocData/259`, `257`, non-JP variants) and requires a table on all nine
VS stages. Pack v84 adds the weights and a presence byte to `StageDesc`
(124 to 148 bytes).

`itManagerMakeAppearActor` runs from `grCommonSetupInitAll`, after
`grMainSetupMakeGround`. It needs a non-None appearance rate, non-zero
toggles, a weight table with a non-zero toggled sum, and at least one
`nMPMapObjKindItem` (4) point. More than 30 points halts the game; the
VS stages have 6 to 19. The actor's table covers kinds 0..=19, containers
included, with no explosion sentinel. The first spawn wait is drawn at
creation: `min[rate] + rand(max - min)`, with 0/30/25/20/15/10 seconds and
0/90/75/60/45/30 extra ticks. Each update after `nSCBattleGameStatusWait`
counts the wait down. At zero, if a struct is free, it draws a kind and
then a point, and calls `itManagerMakeItemSetupCommon` with no parent and
zero velocity. It always draws the next wait.

The actor is on link 2 (Item actor) at priority 3. Processes in a
priority run in link order, so it runs after the fighters' priority-4
physics and before the item link's main processes.

Training clears `item_toggles` (`sc1PTrainingModeFuncStart`), so it has
no actor and an empty container table. VS keeps
`dSCManagerDefaultBattleState`: all toggles set, middle appearance. The
actor's first draw moves every later VS draw. The CPU scenes therefore
diverge from their old goldens.

## Container contents

`itMainMakeContainerItem` gives the selected kind velocity
`(0, llITCommonDataContainerVelocitiesY[kind], 0)`. That is 26 for every
kind except the Beam Sword, which is 40. The item projects from the parent
item's collision. On success the parent gets the fast appear spin.
`itBoxCommonCheckSpawnItems` makes 1, 2 or 3 items from
`dITBoxItemSpawnVelocities`. One time in 32 they are identical. Otherwise
later picks use the table without the sentinel. Each pick happens between
the makers, as the source does. `itManagerMakeItemSetupCommon` emits the
spawn swirl and sets the slow appear spin: `spin_speed * 10°`, unsigned.

Only the Tomato, Heart and Star have makers. The other 13 utilities still
take their draws, so the RNG stream matches the source, but their maker
makes nothing. A container that picks one still counts as opened.

## Tomato and Heart

File 251 + 0xB8 and + 0x100. They are light consume items with no attack
(`nGMAttackStateOff`) and hit status none. Their models are file 86 +
0xAB0 and + 0x1158; the Heart's attributes set `is_display_xlu`. The Wait,
Fall and Dropped statuses use `itMapCheckDestroyDropped`. Gravity and
terminal velocity are 1.2/100 for the Tomato and 0.25/30 for the Heart.
Rebounds are 0.3/0.5 for the Tomato and 0.1/0 for the Heart. Only the
Tomato spins (100%).

`ftCommonLightGetProcDamage` eats a held consume item. It runs at the end
of `LightGet`, on damage during `LightGet`, and when `LightGet` loses its
floor. `ftParamSetHealDamage` adds 100 or 999 to `damage_heal` and starts
colour animation 9. `ftMainProcUpdateInterrupt` then heals one percent a
frame and stops at zero percent. When it stops, it ends animation 9. The
item is destroyed from the hand, so there is no dust.

## Star

File 251 + 0x148. It is a touch item that interacts only with fighters,
and it cannot clank, hop, reflect or shield. Its model is file 86 + 0x1560
with a material table. It is made moving 8 units/frame towards the
camera's look-at X with 50 upwards. Its attack turns on after 16 ticks
(`multi`). Every floor contact sets the Y velocity to 50, and walls and
ceilings rebound at 1.0, so it never rests. In `ftMainUpdateDamageStatItem`
a touch item takes no damage path. The Star turns its attack off, sets
`hit_normal_damage = 1`, and calls
`ftParamSetStarHitStatusInvincible(fp, 600)`. `itStarCommonProcHit` then
destroys it. `star_hitstatus` joins the normal-body checks and
`ftParamGetBestHitStatusAll`. Its timer runs after the invincible timer.
When the timer runs out it ends colour animation 0x4A. Death clears it
(`ftCommonDeadResetSpecialStats`). The Star and Hammer music is not
ported.

The same branch heals a fighter that touches the ground Chansey (`GLucky`)
by its attack damage. The port had sent Chansey through the damage path.
`trainingchansey` changes as a result.

## Drawing

All three makers add XObj kind 0x2E to their root. The root is descriptor
1 once `itManagerMakeItem` ejects descriptor 0. `psp-game` draws that
node's mesh as a camera-facing quad, turned by `rotate.z`. Held, it draws
at the item position the pool keeps at the item-light joint. Descriptor
1's own translate is not added under the joint. The Star's material
animation is not played.

## N64 reference

A temporary decomp build booted Training on Dream Land with two Marios.
It made a Tomato at Mario + (75, 300), a Heart at (-500, 600) and a Star
at (500, 300) at the end of `sc1PTrainingModeFuncStart`. Route: A at
frame 330. The patch was reverted and the decomp rebuilt to SHA-1
`e2929e10…`. The N64 matches the PSP `trainingutility` scene. The Tomato
lands behind Mario and the Heart drifts down to his left. The Star bounces
left, touches Mario and is spent, and Mario flashes. After A, Mario picks
the Tomato up and it is gone a few frames later. The two runs press A at
different points in the item timeline, so frames were compared by event,
not by tick.
