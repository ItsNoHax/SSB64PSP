# RE-476 — The opening's slowdowns: background scene loads and the room's frozen picture

Status: IMPLEMENTED (PSP-2000 PSPLink profile runs of the opening at the XMB budget before and after the background loads; the user's XMB `boot.log`; PPSSPP profile runs of the opening and the attract loop; frame-by-frame hash A/B of the attract loop against `ee67b6a`; golden matrix. The room transition's fix is measured on the PSP by bisection; its after-run on the PSP is pending: the PSP left USB mid-batch)
Topics: hardware, performance, loading, memory, psp-ge, framebuffer, pack, platform
Relevant files: `psp-runtime/src/{scene_files,assets,gu,profile,movie,meshdraw}.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/src/residency.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/{auto_demo,opening/room}.rs`, `psp-game/src/{main,scene_load,opening_screen}.rs`, `psp-game/Cargo.toml`, `refs/ssb-decomp-re/src/mv/mvopening/mvopeningroom.c`

## Question

From the XMB the production build (pack v107, `ee67b6a`) boots on the
user's PSP-2000, "but there are some performance slowdowns still during
the intro": the N64 logo, the opening's 19 scenes and the title. The game
advances one tick per drawn frame, so a frame over 16.7 ms plays as
slowdown. What is slow, and how can it be hidden without changing a tick
or a pixel?

## Hardware and builds

  checkout as `host0:`. Each run: `reset`, `cd ms0:/PSP/GAME/ssb64/`,
  `ldstart` of a `capture_scene_file,profile,memory_ballast` PRX with

- The user's own XMB launches of `ee67b6a` with `boot_log`

## Findings

### 1. Scene loads read in the frame the scene started

Every opening transition read the next scene's files in one frame
(RE-475): on the PSP-2000 0.04–0.52 s a scene (user's `boot.log`: the Run
scene 5.7 MB in 0.52 s, Jungle 5.6 MB in 0.52 s, Clash 5.3 MB in 0.50 s),
and each opening fight read its figatrees again when its battle started
30 ticks later (0.05–0.08 s). Two further faults made it worse:

- **The held picture re-read the last scene.** An opening scene that
  exits before the next one's music tic holds its last picture, drawn
  again, for about 15 ticks (`opening_screen::hold`, RE-467). The next
  scene's files were entered at the exit, so the last scene's were
  dropped after that frame and the held picture loaded them again on
  demand, mid-hold: 0.4–5.4 MB per transition (`boot.log`: "loaded on
  demand: 10 files 5438138B" during the newcomers' hold of the clash).
- **Lists missed the figures' figatrees.** The opening's figures play
  figatrees the N64 reads per status into each figure's figatree heap
  (`mvOpeningRoomFuncStart`'s `desc.figatree_heap`); the scene lists held
  only their main files, so each status change read one file mid-scene
  (the room's random figures, the runners, the clash's fighters).

### 2. The room's frozen picture on the GE

At tic 1037 the room's default camera stops filling and the transition
opens over the last picture, which the port draws from a 300×220 capture
(`Gpu::draw_frozen_picture`). On the PSP-2000 ticks ~1080–1376 ran at
20–28 FPS in both builds with 25–30 ms of GE wait a frame. Skipping each
part of the picture in turn (a temporary probe build, `opening@1340`):

| Skipped | FPS (ticks 1080–1340) | GE wait (ms, average) |
|---|---|---|
| nothing | 21.3, 19.9 | 24.3, 28.8 |
| the frozen picture | 59.4, 59.9 | 0.5, 2.8 |
| the transition's outline model | 21.3, 19.9 | 24.3, 29.1 |
| the transition's overlay model | 21.3, 19.9 | 25.1, 29.8 |
| the wallpaper sprite | 21.3, 19.9 | 21.1, 25.2 |
| the whole movie draw | 30.6, 29.0 | 26.1, 28.6 |

One bilinear 32-bit quad of about 340×250 pixels sampled from main
memory cost the GE about 27 ms a frame.

### 3. The residency lookup

`Pack::blob`'s span lookup (RE-475) is 0.5% of all samples of `vs4@4000`
under PPSSPP's sampling profiler, about 0.08 ms a frame. A four-entry
recent-span cache measured the same (0.6%) and was not kept. Registering
a read's spans one sorted `Vec::insert` at a time, and dropping a scene's
spans one `remove` at a time, was the cost that showed: about 5 ms for the
title demo's 358 files.

## Changes

- **Background reads** (`scene_files::prefetch`): the files of the scene
  expected next are read with `sceIoReadAsync` into cache-line-aligned
  blocks while the current scene runs. `safe_point`, after each frame,
  completes the read in flight, writes the block back from the data cache,
  registers its files and starts the next read: the registry and the
  allocator stay on the game thread. Reads are 1 MB at most and stop being
  queued with less than 6 MB free (`psp`'s allocation-error handler never
  returns, RE-475).
- **Entering a scene** keeps the files a prefetch read for it, waits for
  any read still running that it wants, and drops a prefetch for another
  scene (a skipped opening). The wait is the only forced wait: it
  lengthens that frame and never changes a tick; under PPSSPP and on the
  PSP-2000 every opening and attract-loop scene's prefetch finished in
  time (`wait_us=0`). A battle the scene starts (an opening fight, How to
  Play, the title demo) leaves the next scene's prefetch running
  (`enter_within`).
- **What is read ahead.** The opening's order is fixed: the logo reads
  the room, each opening scene the next, the newcomers the title. The
  attract loop's too: the title reads How to Play's files, How to Play the
  characters', the characters the title demo's stage (its order is fixed,
  `auto_demo::next_gkind`) and every fighter it can field (players 3 and 4
  come from the random number generator at the demo's start; the ones it
  does not draw are dropped then), the demo the N64 logo's.
- **An opening scene's files are entered when it begins**, after the hold
  that still draws the last one (`opening_screen::load`), not at the
  last scene's exit.
- **Scene lists.** The opening figures' figatrees, the opening fights' and
  How to Play's battle files are in their scenes' lists. The room's two
  random figures are picked when its files are read ahead
  (`Room::pick_figures`, from `osGetTime`'s low byte as before, earlier:
  a random draw either way) and its list names them.
- **The pack stays open** for the run (one descriptor for background
  reads, one for reads that wait): an open cost about 10 ms under PPSSPP.
  A failed read reopens it once (a descriptor does not survive standby).
- **Registry updates** go in one move per read (`residency::insert_run`)
  and one pass per scene change (`residency::remove_where`); figatree
  lists come from one scan of the animation table.
- **The frozen picture is sampled from VRAM**: the GE copies the capture
  (the whole padded 512×256 buffer) into a VRAM block once per capture
  (`sceGuCopyImage`) and draws from there. The capture computes each
  column's source pixel once rather than per pixel (14.8 → 4.8 ms of CPU
  under PPSSPP on that frame). VRAM now holds the two colour buffers, the
  depth buffer and this 512 KiB photo (1.9 of 2 MiB).
- **Profiler**: `io`, `demand`, `files` (the safe point, counted in the
  frame's CPU time), the movie draw's `mpose`, `mmesh`, `skin`,
  `msprites`, and the mesh lists' `nodemat`, `material`, `submit` and
  `prims`; `load` and `prefetch` lines, written at the frame's end.
  `frame_hash` logs each finished frame's pixel hash.

What remains under 60 on the PSP is CPU work: the Run, Yoster/Sector and
Clash scenes take 12–14 ms of CPU a frame (2-second averages; PPSSPP
counts under 4 ms), almost all in the movie draw's mesh lists (`mmesh`
6–12 ms: CPU skinning 1.0–1.4, node matrices 1.0–1.5, material state
1.2–2.0, draw submission 0.5–1.1, the rest the per-primitive vertex
expansion into uncached list memory and the node walk). PPSSPP's clock
counts instructions, so uncached stores, cache misses, divisions and
system calls cost far more on the PSP than it shows: the opening's
median frame is 2.1 ms of CPU under PPSSPP and 7.8 ms on the PSP-2000.

### Memory

PPSSPP, the attract loop with `memory_ballast` at the XMB's 47,891,712
bytes: lowest free 24.9 MB (largest block 24.3 MB), with a scene's files
and the next one's held together; no prefetch was cut short. Without
ballast the lowest free memory fell from 34.9 MB (RE-475's opening) to
30.2 MB over the whole loop. On the PSP-2000 at the XMB budget the
opening's lowest free was 27.9 MB (33.0 MB before).

## Verification

- **Frame hashes**: `golden_capture,frame_hash` builds of this batch and
  of `ee67b6a` (the same hook applied) under PPSSPPHeadless: all 11,602
  frames of the attract loop from the N64 logo through the opening, the
  title, How to Play, Characters and the title demo to the next N64 logo
  hash identical (10,504 distinct hashes); `opening@1600` again after the
  frozen-picture change (1,599 frames identical).
- Golden matrix 198 of 198 without a rebaseline; deepest game stack 239,808 bytes; peak memory 17.0 MB (`vsnocontest`).
- Host: 2,173 workspace tests on one thread with `SSB64_ROM`; clippy (`cargo +1.98.0 clippy`, warnings denied); rustfmt. The production EBOOT (no `boot_log`, no debug info, 4,201,482 bytes, SHA-256 `511945e4…47a2`) plays the opening under PPSSPP.
- `residency::tests::spans_resolve_and_misses_load` covers the batched
  insert and removal; `auto_demo::tests::candidate_kinds_hold_every_fighter_init_fields`
  checks the demo's prediction against `init` over 64 seeds.

## Deviations

- The room's figures are picked when the logo starts reading the room's
  files rather than in `mvOpeningRoomFuncStart`: the same clock byte
  draw, earlier.
- D-046's deviations, plus: a scene's files and the next one's are held
  together while the next is read ahead.
- No tick, value or pixel changes (frame hashes above).
