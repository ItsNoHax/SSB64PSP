# RE-341 — RE-339/RE-340 validated against the ROM, PPSSPP, N64 RDRAM and a PSP-2000

Status: COMPLETE (ROM, pack v37, PPSSPPHeadless, Mupen64Plus RDRAM, PSP-2000 scripted scenes and timing; hand-input hardware checks not run)
Topics: asset-pipeline, rendering, texture, memory, relocations, hardware, timing, visual-regression, fighter
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-rom/src/strict.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/src/scene_deps.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/src/reloc_link.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/src/archive.rs`, `tools/romtool/src/main.rs`, `psp-game/src/main.rs`, `psp-game/src/play.rs`, `crates/ssb-capture/src/lib.rs`, `tests/golden/scenes.tsv`

**Question.** 69ed3a0 (RE-339, RE-340) was host-tested only. Do its pieces
hold against the ROM, a built pack, PPSSPP, the original game's RDRAM and
the PSP-2000?

**Integration.** 69ed3a0 was built on 5c659db, beside six unpushed local
commits that already used RE-333–RE-338. The merge renumbers its records
RE-339 and RE-340 and adds stale motion IDs for Samus, Link, Yoshi and
Captain Falcon from their `ft<name>status.h` tables.

**Strict mode.** Over pack v37, `romtool strict` reports 0 unresolved.
The node-parent rule is not a false positive: no node in any object names
a later or foreign parent. The check did not cover what `meshdraw`
resolves through material animations, so it now also checks each
`MatAnimDesc` script, palette range and sprite list, every animated
palette's CLUT, and every two-tile blend's material animation and
`TextureIDNext` textures (103, 321 and 2 entries; 0 unresolved). Four
single-word corruptions of a pack copy (a primitive's texture, a sprite, a
palette range, a blend texture) each produce exactly one issue. The
`strict_render` `psp-game` build reaches Training: its grab frame matches
`f1-training-grab` exactly. Over the corrupted pack it draws only
`BG_TRAINING_STRICT_FAILED` (200, 0, 100).

**Scene residency.** `romtool scene-deps` now takes `--fighter
<kind>[:<costume>]`; `fighter_object` moved into `ssb_rom::scene_deps`.
Bytes are texels of all packed mip levels plus CLUTs.

The search was exhaustive over the nine VS stages and every costume of the
12 playable fighters. Donkey Kong's 15 textures per costume are 32-bit
direct colour with packed mip chains (the 384×96 body texture alone is
349,184 bytes), and each costume has its own set. Training fits the
~700 KiB budget; four-player VS scenes and Hyrule Castle alone do not.
RE-076's 217 KiB estimate predates these packs. Mip levels are PSP-side
(RE-053); the N64 never samples them (RE-127).

**Extern linker.** `reloc_link::alloc_size` reproduces
`lbRelocGetAllocSize` from the file table and the ROM's `u16` extern-ID
arrays, without decompression (`Archive::extern_ids`). For 13 closures
(fighter mains 203, 213, 217, 225, 236, 247; model 296; stage files 104,
106, 113, 152; and two multi-root sets), `romtool link` uses exactly the
reserved size less the last file's alignment slack. For order, a
Mupen64Plus probe read `sLBRelocInternBuffer`'s status buffer (US
`0x800D62E0`, file id → address) every 10 frames while the ROM idled for
3,600 frames through its intro and fighter showcases. `layout_after`,
which skips files already resident as `lbRelocFindStatusBufferFile` does,
predicts all 448 placements of 185 roots in 15 distinct snapshots exactly.
Ignoring residency instead gives 35 mismatched roots, so the comparison
can fail.

**Goldens.** After the merge all 69 goldens matched: the three `psp-game`
scenes land no hit, so neither handicaps nor staling can move them (the
hardware probe below reads dummy damage 0; the scripted jab no longer
reaches the dummy as RE-295 found). New scenes `costume1`–`costume3` pick
C-Right, C-Down and C-Left on the Training entry with Fox as the player;
each differs from a C-Up control by 916 pixels at 2×, all on Fox, and
repeats exactly. The dummy then takes `mnPlayers1PTrainingGetFreeCostume`:
the source fills the player's slot, gives the CPU slot the first royal
costume the player is not wearing and denies a pick of that costume. With
Mario on both sides the dummy wears costume 1. Exactly the three
Mario-dummy goldens changed and were rebaselined: `f1-training-fireball`
2,124, `-shadows` 432, `-grab` 2,296 pixels at 2×, each confined to the
dummy. 72 of 72 then match twice.

The grab holds the dummy at the same place, and Fox wears the same three
costumes, as in PPSSPP; the differences are edge and filtering noise of
the size RE-326 recorded. A temporary timing PRX (`6bee2d64…`, pre-staling
code, removed) ran the Fireball route without freezing for 3,600 Training
ticks. Simulation took 1,780 µs/tick in ticks 1–600 and 1,740–1,741 µs in
each later window; the whole loop took 33,364 µs/tick in every window. So
`psp-game`, which ticks once per loop, runs Training at 30 Hz on the PSP-2000.
RE-329's 1,026 µs/tick is the viewer's fixed-step stage scene, not this
loop.
