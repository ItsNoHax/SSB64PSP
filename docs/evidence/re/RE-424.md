# RE-424 — The lighting bit decides lighting, and a cutout keeps its coverage

Status: IMPLEMENTED (decomp source, ROM display lists, N64 RDRAM display-list walks of 657 frames from warp-boot builds covering all twelve fighters, their electric skeletons, neutral/up/down specials and VS entries, 20 items, eight VS stages, the opening room and the Training select; angrylion-rdp-plus source for the RDP coverage rules; romtool trace test; PPSSPPHeadless goldens captured twice and broken by each rule; no physical PSP)
Topics: rendering, lighting, fighter, alpha, blending, texture, asset-pack, visual-regression
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-rom/src/{mesh,pack,wide_tile}.rs`, `tools/romtool/src/main.rs`, `psp-runtime/src/meshdraw.rs`, `tests/golden/*.png`

## Questions

1. RE-423 let a list's own `G_LIGHTING` state decide shading, which
   changed vertices of Samus, Kirby, Jigglypuff and Ness. Does every
   fighter vertex, in every costume and special part, load under the
   lighting state the N64 loads it under?
2. 29 lists never stated `G_LIGHTING` and still read a colour whose bytes
   look like a unit normal as a normal (RE-021). What state do they really
   inherit?
3. Planet Zebes's upper platforms show a yellow ring the N64 does not.
   What do the `TEX_EDGE` render modes do on the RDP, and what is the
   closest GE equivalent?

## Method

- **Warp-boot builds** (`scmanager.c`, `ftdisplaymain.c`,
  `sc1ptrainingmode.c`, reverted afterwards; the rebuilt ROM's SHA-1 is
  `e2929e10…`). Training on Dream Land boots directly; the fighters are
  byte-patched into `dSCManagerDefaultSceneData` (with `n64crc.py`), so
  one build gives every pairing. A byte at 0x80780000 forces
  `fp->colanim.skeleton_id` in `ftDisplayMainDrawAll` (the electric
  skeletons); a byte at 0x80780010 spawns an item with
  `itManagerMakeItemSetupCommon`. A second hook boots a four-player VS
  battle (`gSCManagerTransferBattleState`) for the entries. Scene bytes
  select the opening room and the Training select.
- **Captures.** Six Training pairs (idle, skeleton sets 1 and 2); twelve
  runs with each fighter as the player pressing B, up+B and down+B, dumped
  every 4 frames for 48 frames; 20 items; three VS groups of four through
  their entries, every 5 frames; the RE-422 frame-414 dumps of eight
  stages; 25 opening-room and 7 Training-select frames.
- **Walk.** From `gSYTaskmanDLHeads[0] - 0x10` (the task's prologue and its
  branch into head 0), following calls and branches and tracking the
  geometry mode, other mode, PRIM and ENV, every `G_VTX` is recorded with
  its vertex address, count and `G_LIGHTING` bit. Files are located through
  `sLBRelocInternBuffer`'s status buffers (id and address).
- **Port side.** `mesh::trace_vertex_loads` runs the converter with a
  record of each `G_VTX`; `romtool lighting` lists every load of every
  scene graph, electric skeleton set and copy hat as the packer converts
  them, and the comparison matches loads by file, vertex offset and count.

## Findings

- **The fighters.** Under the RE-423 rules every idle and special load of
  all twelve fighters matched the N64: the joint lists that clear
  `G_LIGHTING` restore it, so their vertices between the two are the only
  unlit ones, and the RE-423 change for Samus, Kirby, Jigglypuff and Ness
  was right. Costumes change only `MObj` palettes and colours
  (`lbCommonAddMObjForFighterPartsDObj`), and `gcDrawMObjForDObj` never
  touches the geometry mode, so they load the same way.
- **Kirby's electric skeleton did not match.** Its body list (328 +
  0x198F8) clears `G_LIGHTING`, then calls its `MObj`, which writes
  `LIGHT_1`/`LIGHT_2` with `gSPLightColor`. The N64 loads the next 13
  vertex runs unlit; the port read a light colour write (`MObj` or
  `G_MW_LIGHTCOL`) as turning lighting on (RE-105) and shaded them. The
  RSP's lighting bit is set only by `G_GEOMETRYMODE`
  (`objdisplay.c:1210-1216` writes the colours alone).
- **Every object starts lit.** Each frame runs `sSYRdpResetDisplayList`,
  which clears `G_LIGHTING`, and then the scene's lights function
  (`scVSBattleFuncLights`, `sc1PTrainingModeFuncLights`,
  `mnVSResultsFuncLights`, the menus' and movies' lights lists) sets it;
  nothing in `objdisplay.c`, `ef`, `wp`, `it`, `gr` or `lbtransition.c`
  touches it. In every trace the first list of every object (stage layer,
  item, weapon, effect, entry, fighter part) runs lit, and only lists after
  one of the same object's own clears run unlit.
- **The 29 lists, and those the light-colour rule hid.** The shape guess
  was reached by Mario's, Fox's, Link's, Samus's, Kirby's and Ness's model
  parts (runtime part swaps, lit on the N64), the entries (file 161, 349,
  350, 353, 355, 356), weapons (336 + 0x168), the polygon fighters, the
  emblems (35), the transitions (39–51), the movies (52, 61, 62, 67–73)
  and the bonus stages. Where it disagreed with the N64: Fox's entry Arwing
  (161 + 0x2920, colour-shaped quads the N64 lights: 192 loads) and, under
  the v75 light-colour rule, Captain Falcon's entry car (350 + 0x5C60,
  cleared: 192), the items (86 + 0x3E0, cleared: 5) and the opening room
  (52 + 0x1BFC8: 8).
- **`TEX_EDGE` on the RDP** (angrylion-rdp-plus `combiner.c`, `blender.c`,
  `zbuffer.c`, `coverage.c`). `CVG_X_ALPHA` scales coverage by the
  combined alpha, `cvg' = (alpha * cvg + 4) >> 8`, and with `AA_EN` (every
  `TEX_EDGE` mode in the archive: `0x553078`, `0x553048`, `0xC4113078`,
  `0x0C193078`, `0x0C193048`) a pixel with no coverage left is not written:
  `alpha < 32` at full coverage. A kept pixel over a fully covered
  background overflows coverage, so the blender passes its colour
  unblended (`blend_en` needs no overflow without `FORCE_BL`) and
  `CVG_DST_CLAMP` stores `cvg' - 1`; the VI (mode 0, antialias and
  resample, read from `sSYSchedulerCurrentViMode` in RDRAM) then mixes the
  partially covered pixel with the background by that coverage. The
  filter mixes the TLUT's transparent entry's colour into the edge texels
  on the N64 too (bilinear after the TLUT), so the yellow is there, but at
  low coverage.
- **Emulators.** Rice draws the platform with a dark edge, as if its
  texture cache cleared transparent texels, and draws Saffron City's thin
  railings solid; it models neither coverage nor the VI filter. GLideN64
  and angrylion could not be started headless. The RDP rules above decide.

## Implementation

- **Lighting** (`mesh.rs`). `material.lit` is the geometry mode's
  `G_LIGHTING` bit and nothing else; light colour writes keep their
  colours only. `MeshVertex::lit_known` and the shape fallback in
  `pack::add_mesh` and `wide_tile` are gone. `InitialMaterial::SCENE`
  (lit) seeds every graph `romtool` does not seed otherwise, and discovered
  lists; the layer, weapon, shield and transition seeds are lit too.
  `looks_like_unit_normal` remains a census predicate.
- **Cutout** (`pack::alpha_gate`, `meshdraw`). The cutout gate is
  `alpha >= TEX_EDGE_MIN_ALPHA` (32), combined with a threshold as the
  larger bound; a cutout primitive that does not already blend is blended
  by its alpha. The filter compensation trains against the new gate
  (`material_alpha_state`).
- **Two-tile blend** (`romtool::lod_blend_desc`). The proof that pass 1's
  gate cannot reject now bounds the blended alpha by the script's largest
  `PRIM_LOD_FRAC` (`MatAnimData::max_lod_frac`): Dream Land's pond reaches
  114, so the blend never falls below 141 and keeps its two passes.
- **Tools.** `romtool lighting <rom> [--file N]` and
  `mesh::trace_vertex_loads`.
- **Pack v76**, 34,589,248 bytes, SHA-256
  `750508e7fc1f769e44950366430456437e25c425b56f7a69023bed8e22d3c01a`,
  built twice with identical bytes; layout unchanged.

## Verification

- **N64 traces.** After the change the packer's lighting matches all
  76,191 traced loads of scene graphs and all 271 skeleton-set loads; v75
  disagreed on 218 (Kirby's skeleton, Falcon's car, the items, the opening
  room) and the shape guess without the light-colour rule on 192 more (the
  Arwing). The loads the port has no graph for are model-part swaps
  `psp-game` does not port (lit on the N64, and lit under `SCENE`) and the
  special-cased weapon lists (Fireball, blaster, Charge Shot), which clear
  lighting themselves.
- **Test.** `romtool::tests::packed_lighting_matches_the_n64_traces` pins
  seven traced loads; flipping one expectation fails it.
- **Zebes fringe.** Yellow pixels (R > 150, G > 120, B < 100) in the
  upper-platform crop: 1,076 before, 700 with the 32 gate alone, 24 with
  the gate and the blend; the N64 frame has 54 in the same area (its lamps).
- **Goldens.** 76 rebaselined (`docs/visual-regression/README.md`):
  `r2-kirby-skeleton` (50,596, the skeleton's unlit colours),
  `r2-stage-bonus3-race-to-the-finish` (10,144, colours after 149 +
  0x53D8's clear and cutout edges) and 74 cutout-edge goldens (20 to
  11,164: trees, fences, grates, rope, flames, fighter parts, Zebes's
  platforms). 136 of 136 match twice.
- **Negative tests.** The old light-colour rule changes `r2-kirby-skeleton`
  (50,596); an unlit scene seed changes 35 goldens (opening room 79,384,
  results emblem 63,304, the bonus stages, Sector Z); no cutout blend
  changes 74. The 32 gate without the two-tile bound dropped the pond's
  second pass (`f1-vs-pause` 51,112 before the bound).
- **Gates.** Workspace tests with an absolute `SSB64_ROM` and one thread
  pass (1,560). Pinned 1.98.0 Clippy with warnings denied, `cargo fmt --all
  --check` and both PSP release builds pass (the viewer's existing
  warnings remain). The release `run` is 20,480 bytes; the largest frame is
  still `enter_training` at 159,456 bytes, then `EffectVisuals::sync_ko` at
  95,368.

## Deviations

- The GE has no coverage: polygon edges count as fully covered, the edge
  mix is by alpha rather than eighths of coverage, and it blends with what
  is drawn before the pixel, where the VI mixes the finished neighbours.
  A cutout drawn before the surface behind it keeps that surface out of
  its edge pixels (depth is written, as on the N64).
- Lists the traces did not reach (transitions, movies other than the
  opening room, menus other than the Training select, bonus stages) rely on
  the decomp's lights functions; their scenes set `G_LIGHTING` and no
  object between clears it without its own list doing so.

## Conclusion

The RSP lights a vertex when `G_LIGHTING` is set and never because a light
colour was written or a colour looks like a normal. Every scene sets the
bit before its objects draw, so a list that never states it runs lit. With
those two rules the packer matches every traced N64 vertex load, fixing
Kirby's electric skeleton, Falcon's entry car, Fox's entry Arwing, the
items and the opening room, and the shape guess is gone. A `TEX_EDGE` pixel
survives at alpha 32 and the VI mixes its partial coverage with the
background; the GE's alpha test at 32 and blend by alpha remove Zebes's
yellow ring.
