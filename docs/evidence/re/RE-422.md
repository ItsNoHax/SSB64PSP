# RE-422 — Battle draw order by display link and task head; the Training CPU's costume

Status: IMPLEMENTED (decomp source, RDRAM display-list traces and layer toggles from warp-boot builds on eight VS stages, N64 captures through the real Training select, packed-asset ROM tests, host tests, PPSSPPHeadless goldens captured twice and broken by restoring the old order and the old pack; no physical PSP)
Topics: rendering, stage, depth, blending, draw-order, asset-pack, costume, visual-regression
Relevant files: `psp-game/src/main.rs`, `psp-runtime/src/{meshdraw,particles,gu}.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/src/{mesh,pack}.rs`, `tools/romtool/src/main.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/effect.rs`, `crates/ssb-capture/src/lib.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/tests/stage_layer_heads.rs`, `tests/golden/*.png`

## Questions

1. The port drew all four stage layers before the fighters. In what order
   does the N64 draw the stage layers, the fighters, the weapons and items,
   the effects and the translucent lists, and with what depth state?
2. The port's Training CPU (Mario against Mario) wears the yellow costume.
   Which costume does the N64 give it?

## Source

- **Passes.** `gmCameraDefaultProcDisplay` sets `camera_mask` six times and
  calls `gcCaptureCameraGObj` and `syTaskmanUpdateDLBuffers` after each:
  links 1–2, 4, 6/7/9/10/11/12, 13–15, 16–18 and 19–20.
  `gcCaptureCameraGObj` shifts the mask right from bit 0, so each pass runs
  its links in ascending order, and each link its display objects by
  priority, highest first. `syTaskmanUpdateDLBuffers` branches head 0 to
  head 1 at the end of each pass, so a pass runs every head-0 list of its
  links before any head-1 list.
- **Links.** `dGRDisplayDescs`: layers 0–3 on links 4, 6, 13 and 17.
  Shadows on 7 (`ftShadowProcDisplay`), fighters on 9
  (`FTDISPLAY_DLLINK_DEFAULT`), items on 11, the Zebes acid on 12, weapons
  on 14 (`wpManagerMakeWeapon`), `efDisplayCLDProcDisplay` and the
  priority-2 effects on 15 and 18, particle list 4 on 10, list 1 on 15,
  lists 0 and 2 on 18, list 3 on the interface's 25. `EFDesc::dl_link`: the
  entry effects, the rebirth halo and the impact wave on 10; Slash,
  ShockSmall, DeadExplode, CatchSwirl, ReflectBreak and ItemGetSwirl on 18;
  the other fighter effects on 15. Dream Land's front flowers
  (`grPupupuMakeMapGObj`) are on link 16 with
  `grDisplayLayer3PriProcDisplay`.
- **Depth.** Layers 0, 2 and 3 clear `G_ZBUFFER` and set
  `G_RM_AA_OPA_SURF` on head 0; their `SecProcDisplay` also sets
  `G_RM_AA_XLU_SURF` with `G_ZBUFFER` cleared on head 1. Layer 1 sets
  `G_RM_AA_ZB_OPA_SURF` and `G_RM_AA_ZB_XLU_SURF`. `layer_mask`
  (`MPGroundData` + 0x44) selects `SecProcDisplay`: Zebes 0x2, Mushroom
  Kingdom 0x1, Sector Z 0x3, Saffron City 0xA, the others 0.
- **Costume.** `mnPlayers1PTrainingUpdateFighter` places a puck's fighter
  in `mnPlayers1PTrainingGetFreeCostume`, the first royal costume the other
  slot does not wear, and `mnPlayers1PTrainingInitVars` gives a random CPU
  costume 1 when the player already wears costume 0 of that fighter.
  `sc1PTrainingModeInitVars` copies `training_com_costume` unchanged.
  `mnPlayersVSGetFreeCostume` follows the same rule in VS.

## Implementation

- **Draw order.** `draw_training` draws in the original's passes:
  `meshdraw::draw_stage_links` draws the stage layers and controller
  objects of a link range, `DrawState::heads` filters by task head
  (`Heads::Head0`, `Head1`, `Both`), and the order is: links 0–5 head 0,
  then head 1; links 6–8 head 0; shadows; fighters; link 10 (the egg, the
  entry effects, the halo, the impact wave, particle list 4); items; links
  9–12 head 0 (the acid); links 6–12 head 1; links 13–15 (layer 2); the
  weapons; the link-15 effects, the Thunder segments, the link-15 display
  effects and list 1; links 16–17 head 0; the KO blast, the link-18 display
  effects and lists 0 and 2; links 16–18 head 1; list 3.
  `DisplayKind::dl_link` gives each display effect its `EFDesc` link.
  `draw_items_weapons_effects` draws one `BattlePart` at a time.
- **Pack v74** (34,455,072 bytes, SHA-256
  `2dbc2a68fba81d63c078acc8ea55fa01d1efe0ac19fd577d685fe4bf9eaabdd9`,
  rebuilt twice with identical results; layout unchanged):
  - `flags::HEAD1` (`MeshMaterial::head1`) marks the primitives of every
    head-1 item: 281 of 8,889.
  - `Head1Seed::LayerXlu` seeds layers 0, 2 and 3's head 1 with
    `G_RM_AA_XLU_SURF` and `G_ZBUFFER` cleared: 35 primitives lose
    `DEPTH_TEST` and `Z_BUFFER`.
  - An unlit, untextured primitive whose combined alpha is `SHADE_ALPHA`
    alone (`combiner_alpha_is_shade`) keeps `TRANSLUCENT` and blends as
    `AlphaBlend::Shade`: 11 primitives (the Zebes beam, the Saffron glows
    and haze, the Hyrule shadows, three bonus-stage glows).
- **Pause border.** `Gpu::draw_rect_fill` disables texturing, blending and
  the alpha test before the pause menu's border. The border inherited the
  last draw's texture state and vanished once layer 3 drew last.
- **Costume.** No change: the port already follows
  `mnPlayers1PTrainingGetFreeCostume`, and the capture scenes' CPU wears
  what the N64 gives it after the same picks.
- **Scenes.** `trainingjungle`, `trainingzebes`, `trainingsaffron` and
  `traininginishie`: Training on that stage with no input, at tick 400.

## Deviations

- An effect draws whole at its link's place: the head-1 lists of an effect
  on link 10 or 18 draw before the stage's head-1 lists of that pass, not
  after. Only translucent lists are reordered among themselves, and only on
  Zebes, Sector Z and Saffron City (layer 1) and Saffron City (layer 3).
- Items and fighters draw whole in the fighters' pass; their few head-1
  lists (`itdisplay.c`, `ftdisplaymain.c`) do not wait for the stage's.

## Other differences seen

- Planet Zebes: the tube draws blue where the N64 shows dark teal, and the
  ground near it has no red tint. Saffron City: the door's yellow lights
  are fainter than on the N64. Both are outside the draw order (TODO).
- The N64 Training captures show the Training HUD and the 1P/CP tags,
  which the port does not draw.

## Conclusion

The N64 draws the battle in six passes of display links, each running its
head-0 lists before its head-1 lists. Layers 2 and 3 and Dream Land's front
flowers draw after the fighters with no depth test, so they cover the
fighters and the link-15 effects, and layer 1's translucent lists draw
after the fighters. The port now draws in that order, marks head-1
primitives in the pack, draws layers 0, 2 and 3's head-1 lists with no
depth test, and blends the untextured `G_CC_SHADE` glows. The Training
CPU's yellow costume is what the N64 gives a second Mario.
