# RE-330 — Shared capture links and Donkey Kong cargo carry

Status: COMPLETE (decomp source, host tests, ROM animation cross-check, PSP builds, PPSSPP capture)
Topics: fighter, gameplay, grab, capture, throw, animation, asset-pipeline, visual-regression
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-game/src/grab.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/status.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/src/anim.rs`, `tools/gen-anim-table.py`, `psp-runtime/src/scene.rs`, `psp-game/src/main.rs`

**Question.** How do the common catch, capture and thrown callbacks link two
fighters, and how does Donkey Kong's forward throw turn into cargo carry?

**Source.** `ftcommoncatch*.c`, `ftcommoncapture*.c`, `ftcommonthrown*.c` and
`ftdonkeythrowf*.c` give the status transitions, interrupt priority, breakout
counter and throw flags. The three fighters' `MainMotion.c` files give the
catch boxes, throw hit descriptors and motion-command release frames.
`ftdata.c` pairs common status IDs with motion IDs and the fighters' motion
tables pair those with archive files. Fox's and Donkey Kong's motion tables
contain unbraced null triples; counting only brace groups shifts later thrown
motions. The table generator now groups the flattened words in threes, and
its output is byte-for-byte reproducible from the decompilation.

**Implementation.** `ssb-game` owns the two sides of a grab. A fighter has no
pointer to another fighter, so callbacks queue partner-directed events and
the match loop exchanges them after each fighter tick and after the catch
search in the hit phase. The catcher runs `Catch` → `CatchPull` → `CatchWait`
→ throw; the held fighter runs `CapturePulled` → `CaptureWait` → its thrown
motion. Throw descriptors supply damage and knockback. Shield-grab damage
uses the guard's persistent `is_setoff` bit, as `ftCommonCatchCheckInterruptGuard`
does. Donkey Kong's forward throw lifts into `Shouldered`, then his cargo
statuses handle walking, turning, jumping, falling, landing, damage, throwing
and mash escape. The runtime samples the catcher's `joint_itemheavy_id`
world position for the held fighter. The pack now includes the common grab
and thrown clips for Mario, Fox and Donkey Kong and the eleven Donkey Kong
cargo slots. A scripted `psp-game` scene captures a held dummy.

**Verification.** The focused grab tests exercise catch timing and range,
forward/back throws, shield-grab damage, cargo lift/walk/throw, mash escape
and floor-edge release; cargo keeps a weak hit and drops on a strong one.
`romtool anims --verify` compares 414 finite lengths
against the decompilation; all agree. The generated animation table matches
the checked-in table byte for byte. The ROM-built v36 pack is 22,446,048
bytes (SHA-256 `ab9f9dc2ff37591b6f8fb2cc46c42baceda1122cb99091df2762775d5c75e141`).
All 900 workspace tests and both PSP builds passed at the end of this batch.
The initial grab golden matched two repeat PPSSPP captures but its models
were below the platform. RE-331 documents the rendering correction and the
new 69-scene verification.

**Limits.** A held fighter follows the catcher's sampled heavy-item joint
position, but the held fighter's `TopN` offset and joint rotation are not yet
applied. Hand-joint catch boxes still use the existing root-offset hitbox
convention, and hurtboxes are the existing single root sphere. Mario and Fox
back-throw hitboxes for bystanders require a match with more than two
fighters. Damage to a held fighter and stale-move/handicap modifiers remain
deferred. PPSSPP verifies the current visual scene; it is not physical-PSP
proof.
