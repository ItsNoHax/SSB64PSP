# RE-474 — The figatree clock behind `anim_frame`, and the remaining setters' first frame against N64 traces

Status: IMPLEMENTED (decomp source, N64 Training traces of data-only warp copies of the unmodified US ROM, the RE-466 N64 trace of How to Play, host and ROM tests, PPSSPPHeadless trace of all of How to Play; physical PSP pending)
Topics: fighter, status, animation, motion, gameplay
Relevant files: `refs/ssb-decomp-re/src/ft/{ftanim,ftparam,ftmain}.c`, `refs/ssb-decomp-re/src/sys/objanim.c`, `refs/ssb-decomp-re/src/lb/lbcommon.c`, `refs/ssb-decomp-re/src/ft/ftchar/{ftdonkey,ftkirby,ftcaptain,ftpikachu,ftness}/*.c`, `refs/ssb-decomp-re/src/ft/ftcommon/{ftcommonattack100,ftcommoncapturekirby,ftcommoncaptureyoshi,ftcommoncapturecaptain}.c`, `tools/{gen-anim-table,gen-motion-scripts}.py`, `crates/ssb-game/src/{status,motion/mod,motion/scripts,grab,kirby,kirby_copy,captain,pikachu,ness,link,yoshi,samus,purin,map,capture_kirby,capture_yoshi,item_use,setter_tests}.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/tests/explain_movement.rs`, `psp-runtime/src/scene.rs`

## Question

RE-472 and RE-473 left two parts of the fighter status machine: on the
N64 `anim_frame` returns to 0 at a figatree's end (13 statuses differed in
How to Play), and a list of setters that call `ftMainPlayAnimEventsAll`
had no N64 trace (RE-468's audit). What does the N64's clock do at a
figatree's end, which port code reads which clock, and which of the
remaining setters play their first frame?

## Method

- **Source.** `ftAnimParseDObjFigatree` counts `anim_frame` up by
  `anim_speed`. A `Loop` command sets it to `-anim_wait` (the overshoot, 0
  at whole speeds) and plays on; `End` sets it to `anim_wait` (zero or the
  negative overshoot) and marks `AOBJ_ANIM_END`, which
  `gcPlayDObjAnimJoint` turns into `AOBJ_ANIM_NULL`, so the parse stops
  and the value stands. `ftMainSetStatus` restarts the figatree at
  `frame_begin` only for a motion with a figatree: `motion_id` -1 and
  `anim_file_id` 0 leave the playing one running, -2 also stops
  `ftParamPlayAnim` from parsing it. The motion scripts keep their own
  wait (`script_wait -= anim_speed`), so a script event's time is the
  frames since the status started, not `anim_frame`.
- **Figatree data.** `tools/gen-anim-table.py`'s walk now reports the
  frames to the first `Loop` or `End` and which it is. Of 5,825 motion
  descriptors, 4,415 end, 1,048 loop (every loop jumps back to frame 0),
  294 have no figatree file and 68 have one the walk rejects.
- **N64 traces.** RE-473's warp copy (`dSCManagerDefaultSceneData`, ROM
  `0x42CD0`: Training, the player at `+0x3B`), copies with the CPU at
  `+0x3D` set to each fighter for Kirby's copies, and with Captain Falcon,
  Pikachu and Ness as the player; header CRC recomputed, every copy under
  `~/ppsspp-test/re474/warp/`. Mupen64Plus (M64Py flatpak, offscreen),
  scripted pad, both fighters' status, `anim_frame`, position per frame.
  The base ROMs stayed `e2929e10…` throughout.

## Findings

**The clock.** The model (count by the speed; a `Loop` subtracts the
length, an `End` sets `length - frame` and stops; no figatree keeps the
running one, -2 stops it) reproduces the N64's `anim_frame` on 31,054 of
31,203 frame-to-frame steps across every RE-473 Training trace, both
fighters; the 149 others are status 59 at a fractional speed, which the
check's whole-step prediction does not model.

| How to Play (N64 trace, both fighters, 8,896 samples) | `anim_frame` differs |
|---|---|
| RE-473 build | 1,523 samples in 16 statuses |
| This batch | 19 samples: `Guard` and `GuardSetOff` (below) |

The port keeps two clocks: `StatusState::anim_frame`, the N64's, which
the decomp's own reads take (the dash, run, run-brake, walk-phase,
attack-100 and slap wrap tests, Link's down-air rehit window, the Pipe
yaw, the yubideppou check, every `frame_begin` a ground/air switch passes,
and the paused script's resume), and `StatusState::clock`, the frames
since the status started, which the port's own constructs read: the end
of a status with a known length (`animation_ended`), the script events
the port tests by frame (`crossed` and 45 spawn and flag thresholds), the knee-bend and cargo-landing counters, the idle camera and the
drawn clip's start.

**Setters.** Every status below starts on `anim_frame` 1 on the N64 (a
`ftMainPlayAnimEventsAll` after `ftMainSetStatus`) and lasted the frames
shown; the port did not play any of them.

Not played on the N64 and unchanged: Donkey Kong's `ThrowFWait`,
`ThrowFKneeBend` and `ThrowFFall`, the copied Samus and Donkey Kong
loops, Kirby's wait after a turn, the rapid jab loops. The item throws
and the Barrel and Tornado captures already play in the port (RE-468's
audit listed them); they stay untraced.

## Result

- `anim_frame` matches the N64 in How to Play on all but 19 of 8,896
  samples; positions, statuses, damage, facing and the random generator
  still match on every frame.
- The setters of five fighters, Kirby's copies and the capture victims
  play their first frame, each against an N64 trace; the durations follow
  from the figatree lengths.

  position, damage and facing match the N64 trace on all 4,448 frames for
  both fighters, the generator and its draw count on every frame; 19 of
  8,896 `anim_frame` samples differ (above), against 1,523 before.

- Goldens: 3 of 198 changed, each traced by reverting fixes and

## Deviations and remaining differences

- `Guard`'s and `GuardSetOff`'s `anim_frame` (19 How to Play samples):
  `ftCommonGuardInitJoints` restarts the shield-pose joint animation at
  the stick angle's fraction (`lbCommonAddDObjAnimJointAll`) and plays it;
  the port poses the shield separately and does not keep that clock.
- Donkey Kong's cargo jump and fall play at speed 0 on the N64, so their
  `anim_frame` stands at 0; the port's status timing runs at 1 for their
  counters, so the value counts.
- The idle camera's 120 frames read the status clock, as before; the N64
  reads `status_total_tics`, which `is_control_disable` pauses.
- Untraced: the Barrel and Tornado captures, the item throws (already
  playing), Kirby's Final Cutter landing from the rise, Yoshi's aerial
  release and Link's aerial Boomerang return.
