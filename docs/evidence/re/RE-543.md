# RE-543 — Weapon script windows lost to a whole-file blob; the Thunder Jolts' creation-frame parse

Status: COMPLETE (decomp source, N64 warp-ROM cpufx and roster-A2 traces with GLideN64 screenshots, PPSSPP goldens; pack v121; no hardware run)
Topics: weapon, pack, animation, colanim, entry, n64-trace, goldens
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-rom/src/pack.rs`, `psp-game/src/main.rs`, `tools/romtool/src/main.rs`

## Question

RE-541 and RE-542 left Pikachu's Neutral B open. From cpufx-s el 162 the PSP drew Pikachu faint grey with white arcs at the top left and a blue haze. The causes considered were his `FighterPikachuSpecialN` colour animation, GE state leaking from the burst, and the burst's timing. Separately, Kirby's roster-A2 entry pose looked one frame ahead of the N64. Which is it, for each?

## N64 evidence

All traces come from warp-ROM copies of the baserom (SHA-1 e2929e10). They were run headless with `SDL_VIDEODRIVER=offscreen`.

- **The colour animation matches.** In the cpufx-s rerun with colanim columns (`CA` rows), `FighterPikachuSpecialN` is set at el 161. `is_use_color1` and color1 then cycle every three frames from el 162: `0000ff32`, then `ffffff5a`, then off. The PSP's `colanim` runs the same phase: set on draw tick 559, then blue, white and clear on ticks 560-562.
- **Rice hides the tint.** Rice does not render the `G_RM_FOG_PRIM_A` blend; GLideN64 does. On GLideN64 the tail is tan-grey on the blue frames and pale on the white ones, which the PSP's `set_constant_fog` reproduces. RE-542's "the N64 shows Pikachu opaque yellow" was read from Rice frames.
- **No per-part render mode.** A `JT` dump of Pikachu's part display lists shows no `G_SETOTHERMODE` commands, only combiners. The fog applies to every part, so there is no per-primitive fog gate to port.
- **The ground jolt tree** (cpufx-g2, GLideN64, `WP` kind 10, `wpPikachuThunderJoltGroundProcUpdate`). The weapon lives on el 162-165:

  | el | anim frame | node 1 `TraX` | visible nodes (flags 0) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 162 | 0.0 | -390 | 5 |
  | 163 | 0.5 | -335 | 5 |
  | 164 | 1.0 | -280 | 2, 5 |
  | 165 | 1.5 | -225 | 2, 5 |

  Every other node has flag 2 (hidden). The aerial jolt's only row (el 161, its creation frame) has anim frame 0 and root scale 1.15.
- **The burst's lifetime matches.** GLideN64 cpufx-g2 el 169-172 show the burst large, then small, then gone. They show Pikachu blue-tan, then pale, then opaque, then tan. The PSP's cpufx-s@567-570 are the same frame for frame, so the burst is drawn on el 162-169 on both.
- **Kirby's entry state matches** (roster A2 = `vs4`, `entry2.py` with per-joint DObj flags and anim frames for rec 300-345, F rows identical to the original `vsA2.txt`). On the N64, Kirby's `GObj` anim frame at rec r is r - 271. No joint has a hidden flag in that window. TopN reaches (1421, 907, 0) at rec 331, and the status becomes Wait at rec 391. On the PSP, at tick t = r + 7, Kirby has the same anim frame, the same TopN and position on every tick from 322 to 402, and the same Appear-to-Wait tick (398). The three candidate sites in the handoff (a late `SetFlags`, a skew between the star and pose clocks, the clip's end) are all ruled out at the state level. Against GLideN64 frames 326-334, the remaining visual difference follows the background: the PSP shows less of the sky below the tree at the same Kirby pose. This points to the entry camera, which has not been traced. Static reading found one defect: the PSP entry zoom held the fighter position captured at the end of the previous tick, so the zoom target and the in-bounds test (`gmCameraPlayerZoomFuncCamera`) used a position one tick old. The decomp reads the live TopN each camera tick. Fixed: `EntryZoom` holds the slot and distance only, `tick_battle_camera` resolves the live position (`Camera::tick_player_zoom_in_bounds`). Eight entry-window goldens changed. The N64 camera eye/at trace (rec 320-340) is still needed to confirm the match.

## Cause

- **The pack dropped the weapon script windows.** `PackWriter::add_anim` shared script bytes by archive file id alone. The manager-effect loop packs file 342 (effect 20) and file 335 (effect 35) whole. The weapon loop then packs only a script window of the same files: the aerial jolt (342 + 0x360..0x3F8), the ground jolt (342 + 0x1A20..0x1AE0) and the PK Thunder head (335 + 0x7D20..0x7D70). So each weapon got the whole file's blob with its window-relative offsets.
  - The ground jolt failed on its first event with `UnknownOpcode` (107 at 32). Its seven nodes kept their rest transforms with no flags, so all six spark arcs drew at once at scale 1.5, round the root next to Pikachu. Those were the white arcs and the haze over him. Hiding the ground-jolt draw (experiment E2) made Pikachu opaque yellow and removed the arcs. Hiding his fog (E1) or the burst (E3) did not.
  - The aerial jolt and the PK Thunder head read zeros and stopped after one play.
- **The Thunder Jolts played one step ahead.** `gcAddAnimAll` leaves every DObj at `AOBJ_ANIM_CHANGED`, so its first `gcPlayAnimAll` only parses frame 0 (`objanim.c:137-149`). The jolt's `anim_ticks` already counts that parse (`pikachu_weapon.rs`). The PSP weapon rule, `StageAnimator::start` and then one advancing tick per counted play, therefore drew frame 0.5 (ground) and frame 1 (aerial) on the creation frame.

## Fix

- `add_anim` shares a blob only when the file id **and** the bytes are equal. Each window keeps its own bytes; identical fighter animations stay shared. Pack v121, no layout change. The unit test `anim_blobs_share_only_identical_bytes_of_one_file` covers this.
- `sync_weapons` plays both Thunder Jolts with `PlayRule::ParsedFirst` (`start_changed`, first counted play parses).
- A host probe on the v121 pack shows that both together reproduce the N64 rows above exactly:
  - ground jolt: node 1 at -390, -335, -280, -225, and the same node 2 and node 5 blink;
  - aerial jolt: scale 1.15 at frame 0.

  The PSP trace at draw ticks 560-563 (el 162-165) gives the same values, and the weapon is gone from tick 564.
- The other weapons keep `PlayRule::Weapon` because no N64 row covers them here. The Boomerang, Final Cutter, Link's Spin Attack and items were not affected by the blob bug: their blobs are whole files no one else windows, or windows of files no effect packs.

## Not changed

- The colanim fog path (`set_constant_fog`) matches the N64 colour phase. The experiments gave no reason to change it, or the other fighters' colour animations that share it.
- The burst's lifetime (eight drawn frames, el 162-169) is right. `f1-cpufx-jolt-end` (cpufx-s@569, el 170) pins the frame after it.
- Kirby's entry: see the N64 evidence above. The camera question is recorded in `PLAN.md`.

## Goldens

- The PK Thunder head now pulses its root scale as its script says. The N64 cpufx-u `WP` rows (`wpNessPKThunderHeadProcUpdate`) cycle anim frames 1, 2, 3, 0 with root scale 1.0, 1.0, 0.7551, 1.2 from el 54. The PSP cpufx-u@460-468 shots show the same period and phase (1.2 at 460, 464 and 468; small at 463 and 467). The v120 pack drew one fixed size.
- Rebaselined: `f1-training-pikachu`, `f1-training-pikachu-air`, `f1-cpufx-jolt` (the jolt paths above), `f1-cpufx-up` and `f1-training-ness-thunder` (the PK Thunder head; each differs from the v120 capture only round the head). New row `f1-cpufx-jolt-end`. A v120 build at 3121578 reproduced every old hash, so the diffs come from this change alone.
