# RE-489 — The GE's texel reads cost the CPU 2.2 ms a frame; the per-mesh path has no cheap size win left

Status: COMPLETE (PSP-2000, 6.61 ARK, PSPLink v3.2.1, `profile_hw,capture_scene_file,no_audio` builds of `vs4@2400` at `3dca012` with pack v108 from `ms0:/PSP/GAME/ssb64/`, reports 1–18, two interleaved runs per build; I-cache model in PPSSPP, `tools/icache/`)
Topics: performance, hardware, psp-ge, toolchain
Relevant files: `psp-runtime/src/meshdraw.rs`, `psp-runtime/src/gu.rs`

## Question

RE-488 left two levers open: shrinking the code that one battle draw
pass touches (`draw_magnifiers`, `draw_training` and the per-mesh GE
state), and the GE's main-memory reads, which RE-486 measured at 3.0 ms
of CPU memory stall a frame. Which one is worth a batch?

## Findings

### The rest of the shared draw pass costs no I-cache misses

The I-cache model (RE-482's method, `vs4@2400`, 10–20 s) on `3dca012`:

- `draw_training` (20.0 KB), `draw_frame` (21.4 KB),
  `draw_items_weapons_effects` (40.6 KB) and `draw_magnifiers`
  (10.8 KB) together take under 1% of the draw's misses. They run once
  or a few times a frame, not once per mesh, and nothing hot sits
  between their calls. Outlining them would not show.
- Two thirds of the draw's misses are in four functions, as in RE-482:
  `draw_mesh_vertices` 25%, `draw_object_posed_nodes` 22%,
  `apply_texture_mapping` 10% and `bind_texture` 9%.
- A call to `draw_mesh_vertices` (10.6 KB, `apply_material` inlined)
  runs about 5.1 KB of it. The hot code is spread out: the light
  register and material colour updates, the image, palette and UV
  resolution, the `last_flags` state block, `sceGumDrawArray` and
  `ssb_rom` helpers (prim and index lookup, `alpha_gate`,
  `light_scope`). The cold half is already out of the way and costs no
  fetches. No single block is worth outlining.
- `#[optimize(size)]` on the four functions (nightly
  `optimize_attribute`) is worse in the model: draw-phase misses rise
  45% and fetches 16%. The small helpers (`Pack::node`,
  `Pack::texture`, `sceGuSetMatrix`, `ge_texture_dims`) stop inlining,
  and the hot path spreads over more functions. It was not run on the
  PSP.

### Texel reads are most of the GE's bus contention

A diagnostic build turned the three `sceGuEnable(GuState::Texture2D)`
calls (`bind_texture`, `bind_transition_photo`, `Gpu::draw_photo_rect`) into
`sceGuDisable`. The GE then samples no texels, but every other GE command
and every vertex read is unchanged. PRX SHA-256 prefixes: HEAD
`1a77cb05cb9e6d3e` (byte-identical to RE-488's outlined build), textures
off `94b1dc4b1075438f`. Runs were interleaved: off, HEAD, off, HEAD.

| Build | FPS | CPU frame µs | Draw µs | Frame memory stall µs | Draw memory stall µs | Draw I-miss | Draw D-miss | `ge` wait µs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HEAD, run 1 | 42.03 | 20,594 | 12,653 | 10,884 | 7,835 | 18,406 | 5,713 | 59 |
| HEAD, run 2 | 41.92 | 20,634 | 12,691 | 10,924 | 7,873 | 18,409 | 5,843 | 59 |
| Textures off, run 1 | 48.56 | 18,438 | 10,480 | 8,735 | 5,674 | 18,231 | 5,676 | 40 |
| Textures off, run 2 | 48.47 | 18,456 | 10,498 | 8,754 | 5,691 | 18,233 | 5,740 | 40 |

(Frame and draw are the `hw` spans' cycles at 333 MHz; HEAD reproduces
RE-488's 20,655/12,715 to within 60 µs.)

- Without texel reads the CPU's frame is 2.17 ms shorter, and memory
  stall accounts for all of it: 2.17 ms less a frame, 2.18 ms less in the
  draw. Cache misses barely change (draw I-misses −175, D-misses −70).
  The CPU makes the same misses, and each one waits less.
- That is 72% of RE-486's 3.0 ms, which was all the GE's main-memory
  traffic (texels, the display list and its vertex blocks). Texels are
  most of it.
- The whole frame goes from 42.0 to 48.5 FPS (`prof` frame 23.87 →
  20.65 ms). The CPU's 2.2 ms is most of that; the rest is the GE
  finishing sooner (`ge` 59 → 40 µs, `vblank` 2.75 → 1.70 ms).

## Consequences

- Texture residency in VRAM is the largest measured CPU lever in
  four-player VS. A texture the GE samples from VRAM puts no texel traffic
  on the main bus. With every texture resident the CPU would save up to
  about 2.2 ms a frame. Today's VRAM leaves about 180 KiB free beside two
  32-bit colour buffers (544 KiB each), the 16-bit depth buffer and the
  frozen picture's 512 KiB photo (`docs/memory.md`). RE-426's low-detail
  closure for Dream Land with four fighters is 726,924 bytes. Residency
  therefore needs a hot subset, more VRAM, or both.
- Smaller texel formats (CLUT4/CLUT8 where a texture fits its palette)
  cut the same traffic and need no VRAM. They are worth measuring
  alongside residency.
- I-cache work on the per-mesh path now needs a restructure of how a
  primitive's state is resolved, not more outlining. Expect a few percent
  of draw time per step (RE-487, RE-488).
