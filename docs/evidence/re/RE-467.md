## Question

How do `mnStartup` and the twenty `mvOpening*` scenes run, how do they
reach the title's opening-only layout, and how does the PSP hold their
assets without growing the resident pack?

## Data and pack

- Pack v106. The resident pack is 46,885,168 bytes (SHA-256
  `fceb01eb…dadb`), 89,200 bytes over v105: the opening's 14 fighter clip
  slots (`SLOT_DEMO_RUN` to `SLOT_OPENING1 + 8`), the title's logo plays
  and the room's lists.
- The opening's own files (`llMVOpeningPortraitsSet1FileID` 0x35 to
  `llMVOpeningRunCrashFileID` 0x4B, `opening::is_model_file`) leave the
  resident pack. `romtool pack` builds their objects into
  `MenuScene::OpeningModels` (1,645,532 bytes, 26 objects), loaded when an
  opening scene starts and dropped at the title. `MVCommon` (0x34) stays
  resident, since the ending's room draws from it too.
- `ssb64-menus.pak` (28 scenes) is 13,163,676 bytes (SHA-256
  `48a0fdea…032e`): `Startup`, a pack per opening scene (sprites, the
  scene's script files whole, its camera animations baked one play per
  frame) and `OpeningModels`. The title's pack adds the logo's cutout and
  strikes.
- Sparse `MObjSub` sprite tables in the opening's files (the standoff's
  lightning) fill entries no script selects with the first real one.
- ROM tests: every opening camera bakes to its end; the standoff's
  lightning swaps textures from its sparse table.

## Memory

PPSSPP, capture build, the whole opening from boot, `free` and `max_free`
logged every 30 ticks (`opening tick=… free=… max_free=…`): the lowest is
the Jungle scene (two fighters on Kongo Jungle plus the models pack),
2,570,752 bytes free, 1,569,792 contiguous. The worst existing scene, the
challenger battle, gains the 1,645,532 bytes the models pack took out of
the resident pack.

## N64 comparison

Unmodified ROM from boot (`tools/run-n64-headless.sh`, scratch config,
every sixth frame, with the scene and tic of each frame). The N64's frames
are game frames: its scenes start at their music tics, so it shows fewer
frames than tics in the battle scenes. The PSP counts one tic per frame at
60 FPS, so its captures are compared by scene tic.

- Matches: the N64 logo; the room's logo page, Master Hand pulling and
  dropping the figure, the close-up, the snap and the castle desk
  (capture figures fixed to the reference's Pikachu and Donkey Kong);
  portraits; the Mario, Link, Kirby, Pikachu and Fox scenes; run, cliff,
  Yamabuki, Sector, standoff and clash timing; the newcomers'
  silhouettes; the title's opening layout with its slash.
- The reference's video plugin (Rice) draws neither the room's transition
  (it does not draw into the depth image, so the castle desk appears at
  once) nor the clash's fighters after their camera's depth fill. The
  port follows the source for both.

## Deviations and remaining differences

- Room: the pulled figure's fall lands a few units left of the N64's and
  turns differently in its first frames; the spotlight lacks its soft
  cone; the close-up overlay is lighter.
- Fighters' scenes: Donkey Kong's slap loops once more; Samus's movement
  in the jungle diverges near tic 230; Samus's grapple beam and Yoshi's
  tongue are not drawn; Fox's laser does not turn with the rolled camera.
- Standoff: large ground triangles near the low camera drop out (GE
  clipping of a grid near ±14,000 units). Yoster: the clouds have dark
  fringes and lack the blue tint. The N64 shows a dust puff at Link's
  jump in the run.
- The room's random figures come from `sceKernelGetSystemTimeLow`.
