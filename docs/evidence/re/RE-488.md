# RE-488 — Outlining `draw_stage_links`: 13.6 KB to 0.9 KB, 1.7% less draw time

Status: COMPLETE (PSP-2000, 6.61 ARK, PSPLink v3.2.1, `profile_hw,capture_scene_file,no_audio` builds of `vs4@2400` with pack v108 from `ms0:/PSP/GAME/ssb64/`, reports 1–18, two interleaved runs per build)
Topics: performance, hardware, toolchain
Relevant files: `psp-runtime/src/meshdraw.rs`

## Question

RE-487 left `draw_stage_links` as the largest function in the battle's
shared draw pass: 13,644 bytes and about 850 modelled I-cache misses a
frame (RE-482). The battle calls it once per camera pass and head, between
the fighters' and effects' draws. How much of it runs, and does shrinking
it show on hardware?

## Findings

- The size was inlining, not the per-link loop: `draw_ground_object` and
  `draw_arwing` (each posing its own nodes, four `Mat4::from_trs` calls),
  the still `draw_object` path, the uncached scratch-pose path and
  `LayerPose::compose` were all inlined. On top of these, LLVM unrolled the
  link-range fold over the chained layer and object-link iterators, about
  1.9 KB that runs on every call. The battle uses none of these paths except
  the fold: it always passes a `StagePoseCache`, and Dream Land has no
  controller objects.
- The 4.6 KB scratch `LayerPose` built at the top of every call is not a
  cost: LLVM already sank it (the stack frame was 2,904 bytes).

## Change measured

`draw_stage_links` keeps only the cached-layer path inline. Three new
functions are out of line: `draw_layer_uncached` (cold: the scratch pose or
the still object), `draw_link_objects` (the controller objects and the
Arwing on a link) and `object_link_bounds`. `draw_ground_object`,
`draw_arwing` and `LayerPose::compose` are now `#[inline(never)]`. The link
range is now the layers' fixed bounds widened by the objects' bounds and
clamped to `links`. It may cover more empty links than the old exact fold,
but those draw nothing. This is pure code motion: all 198 goldens match by
pixel hash. `link-order.txt` is unchanged (RE-487).

`draw_stage_links`: 13,644 → 880 bytes. The new out-of-line pieces total
5.3 KB, almost all of it in functions the battle on Dream Land never calls.

## Results

`vs4@2400` without audio. Averages of reports 1–18 are in µs at 333 MHz.
Two runs per build, interleaved A, B, A, B; each build's two runs agree
within 11 µs:

| Build | Frame | Draw | Draw I-miss | Frame I-miss | Draw D-miss | FPS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HEAD `f0f4ed6` | 20,878 | 12,936 | 18,856 | 25,288 | 6,125 | 41.2 |
| Outlined | 20,655 | 12,715 | 18,410 | 24,899 | 5,897 | 41.9 |

HEAD reproduces RE-487's shipped build (20,877 / 12,934 / 18,857) to within
5 µs, so the PSP and pack are unchanged since that record. The draw is 221 µs
(1.7%) faster with 446 fewer I-misses and 228 fewer D-misses a frame. The
whole frame is 223 µs faster.

## Consequences

- The shared pass's large inlined functions are done. What is left of the
  draw's I-cache misses is in the per-mesh path itself (RE-487) and in
  `draw_magnifiers` and `draw_training`, which share the pass.
- On stages with controller objects or Sector Z's Arwing, each object
  now costs one call per pass. This was not measured.
