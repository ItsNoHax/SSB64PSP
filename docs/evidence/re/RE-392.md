# RE-392 — libultra sprites and the battle damage display

Status: IMPLEMENTED (decomp source, ROM decode, host tests, PPSSPPHeadless goldens captured twice, workspace tests, both PSP builds; no physical PSP)
Topics: interface, texture, pack, psp-ge, gameplay, visual-regression
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-rom/src/{sprite,pack}.rs`, `tools/romtool/src/main.rs`, `psp-runtime/src/meshdraw.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/{hud,hud_tests}.rs`, `psp-game/src/main.rs`, `tools/run-ppsspp-headless.sh`

## Question

The battle HUD, the selects and the stage wallpapers draw `SObj`
sprites, which the port has never decoded (RE-193 drew only a captured
framebuffer). How are libultra `Sprite`s stored? And what does the damage
display, the HUD's first element, draw?

## Source

- **`Sprite` and `Bitmap`** (`PR/sp.h`). A `Sprite` is 68 bytes: size,
  scale, `attr`, the primitive colour at +24, a TLUT pointer at +32,
  `nbitmaps` at +40, `bmheight` at +44, `bmfmt`/`bmsiz` at +48, and the
  bitmap pointer at +52. Each 16-byte `Bitmap` is a strip: `width`,
  `width_img` (the stored row length), a buffer pointer at +8 and
  `actualHeight` at +12.
- **Strips.** `lbCommonDrawSObjNoAttr` draws the strips top to bottom,
  `bmheight` rows apart. `lbCommonDrawSObjBitmap` loads each strip with
  `gDPLoadBlock` and no line step, so the ROM stores odd rows already
  interleaved for TMEM:
  - For 4-, 8- and 16-bit texels, the 32-bit halves of each 64-bit word
    are swapped.
  - For 32-bit texels under `SP_TEXSHUF`, the 64-bit halves of each
    128-bit unit are swapped (the decomp's `relocSpriteTool.py`).
- **Combiner.** `lbCommonPrepSObjAttr` picks the combiner by format:
  - I draws the primitive colour through the texel's alpha.
  - IA is `(PRIM - ENV) * TEXEL0 + ENV`, with alpha `TEXEL0 * PRIM`.
  - RGBA and CI use `G_CC_DECALRGBA`.
  - `SP_TRANSPARENT` selects `G_RM_XLU_SURF`.
- **The damage display** (`ifCommonPlayerDamage*`).
  - **Sprites.** File 164 holds twelve IA8 sprites: the ten digits, `%`
    and `H.P`. Their offsets are in `reloc_data.us.h`: 0x148 … 0x1270,
    0x1458 and 0x15D8. The game sets their `attr` to
    `SP_TEXSHUF | SP_TRANSPARENT` at run time.
  - **Layout.** Each player's percent is centred on x
    `{55, 125, 195, 265}`, y 210, with glyph advances from
    `dIFCommonPlayerDamageDigitWidths`.
  - **New damage.** The digits scale by `1 + new / 300` and flash white
    for one frame through `PRIMITIVE, TEXEL0`. They hold for four frames,
    then shrink 0.05 per frame.
  - **Colour.** It fades from the player's colour toward
    `(100, 20, 20)` as damage nears 300%.
  - **A fall.** `ftCommonDeadUpdateScore` starts the break animation.
    Every sixth frame a random glyph starts falling (at 10 units per frame
    upward, then under a gravity of 1). `ftCommonRebirthDownSetStatus`
    stops it.
  - **When it shows.** Training shows it at once. VS shows it at "Go"
    (`ifCommonCountdownThread` at 300 ticks).

## Implementation

- **`ssb_rom::sprite`.** Decodes a `Sprite` through its intern
  relocations into RGBA8888. `combined_image` bakes the format's combiner
  for a PSP `Modulate` draw: I becomes white with alpha I; IA and RGBA
  stay as decoded. `PLAYER_DAMAGE` lists file 164's twelve offsets.
- **Pack v61.** Adds a `SpriteDesc` table:
  - the source file and offset;
  - a normal 8888 `TextureDesc`, clamped;
  - the size, the colour and the ROM `attr`;
  - `TINTED` for I and IA.
  `Pack::sprite(file, offset)` looks a sprite up. The pack is 27,298,032
  bytes, SHA-256 `47f8869d4cf4f90d564b329c5a3779ee7a55c4a4edcc1a1dd5ac253f355bc3df`.
- **`meshdraw::draw_sprite`.**
  - It draws a `GU_TRANSFORM_2D` texture rectangle at N64 screen
    coordinates, mapped onto the pillarboxed viewport.
  - Vertices come from `sceGuGetMemory`.
  - It blends when the live `attr` has `SP_TRANSPARENT`.
  - The white flash uses `TextureEffect::Add` with a white vertex colour,
    which saturates the colour and keeps `alpha * TEXEL0`.
- **`ssb_game::hud::DamageDisplay`.** Ports the update, the break
  animation and the display's glyph list.
- **`psp-game`.**
  - It keeps one display per fighter, made when Training or VS starts and
    again for sudden death, after the fighters are at 300%.
  - It updates the display after each world frame, from the fall and the
    `RebirthDown` status, and shows it once the battle starts.
  - It draws the displays over the 3D scene.

## Deviations

- **Emblem.** The fighter's series emblem behind the digits
  (`fp->attr->sprites->emblem`, coloured by `emblem_colors`) is not drawn
  yet, and neither are the stock icons.
- **Update timing.** The display updates only on frames when the world
  runs, not on frozen frames.
- **Colour on IA sprites.** The combiner's environment colour is taken as
  black, `SObj`'s zeroed default.
- **Coverage.** Only file 164 is converted.

## Verification

- **ROM decode.** A host test decodes the twelve sprites (IA8, 16×19 for
  0, 11×19 for 1, `%` 19×16, `H.P` 22×12). An image dump showed clean
  digits, so the odd-row deinterleave is right.
- **Goldens.** 34 Training and VS goldens changed by 4,604–9,392 pixels.
  Every diff's bounding box lies inside y 454–496 of the 960×544 capture,
  the HUD band. They were rebaselined, capturing twice. `f1-vs-countdown`
  is unchanged, because the display is hidden before "Go".
  - `f1-vs-cpu` shows 3% and 0%.
  - `f1-vs-sudden-death` shows two dark-red 300%.
  - `f1-training-rebirth` shows 0% again after the fall.
- **Timeout.** `vstimeup` now takes over 30 s under a full `-j`, so a
  scene capture's timeout rose to 60 s. A capture still exits on its own.
- **Tests.** Six `hud` host tests, pack round-trip, workspace tests,
  Clippy, both PSP builds. `run` is 119,528 bytes.
