# RE-391 — The VS CPU fights: `ftComputerProcDefault` and its attack tables

Status: IMPLEMENTED (decomp source, host tests, PPSSPPHeadless golden captured twice, workspace tests, both PSP builds; no physical PSP)
Topics: cpu-ai, gameplay, match, visual-regression
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-game/src/computer/{attack,attack_tests,behave,behave_tests,mod,scripts}.rs`, `tools/gen-computer-scripts.py`, `psp-game/src/{main,play}.rs`, `crates/ssb-capture/src/lib.rs`, `tests/golden/f1-vs-cpu.png`

## Question

After RE-390 the VS CPU ran the Stand behaviour. How does the original's
default CPU choose its objectives and attacks?

## Source

- **Setup and trait.** `ftComputerSetupAll` zeroes the state, so `trait`
  and `behavior` start at Default. In the US build the first behaviour
  change waits `1440 - level * 240` ticks, and never less than zero.
  Once that wait runs out, `ftComputerProcessTrait` for the default trait
  picks Default, Unk2, Ally or Captain with `syUtilsRandUShort() & 3`.
  The next change waits 900–1,800 ticks, a quarter of that for Ally.
  `ftComputerProcessBehavior` then sets `objective_base`:
  - Default gives Attack.
  - Unk2 gives Unknown1.
  - Ally gives Ally.
  - Captain gives Patrol.
- **`ftComputerProcDefault`.** It starts from the objective status that
  every behaviour shares. Then it checks, in order:
  - `ftComputerCheckEvadeDistance`: a star-invincible opponent within
    1,500 or a Hammer holder within 2,500 sends the CPU to Evade.
  - `func_ovl3_80135B78`: another fighter's weapon or item hit that will
    reach the CPU within 15 frames sends it to CounterAttack. The chance
    is `(level + 2) / 9`, and Fox and Ness answer with their reflector
    (`is_opponent_ra`).
  - It releases a held Reflector or PSI Magnet, and lets go of the shield
    (script 0x24).
  - It finds the nearest target. Closer than 350 gives Attack. Otherwise
    it tracks an item, or uses a held one, or falls back to
    `objective_base`.
- **The objectives.** Attack, Unknown1 (`func_ovl3_801397F4`), Ally and
  Patrol share one shape:
  - `func_ovl3_8013837C` paces the approach. It locks on to the
    lowest-damage opponent for 600–900 ticks (`ftComputerWaitGetTarget`).
    It waits a level-scaled delay before engaging, and charges or
    releases a neutral special.
  - Within 1,200–1,500 of the target, the CPU may roll behind it
    (`func_ovl3_80138EE4`) or attack (`ftComputerCheckDetectTarget`).
    It chases a target launched upward, walks in bursts, or wanders
    (`func_ovl3_8013877C`).
  - Further away, it fires a projectile special when the way is clear
    (`func_ovl3_80138AA8`), or walks.
  - The objectives differ only in their range weight (0, 0, -0.5, 2.0),
    their chase chance and their burst odds.
- **`ftComputerCheckDetectTarget`.** It walks the fighter's ground or air
  list in `dFTComputerAttackList`. Each row gives an input script, the
  hit's start and end frames, and a detection box. For each row it
  predicts both fighters' positions at the hit frame, under gravity up to
  terminal velocity. It keeps every attack whose box, grown by the
  target's hurtbox, will hold the target. The hurtbox is scaled randomly
  by the CPU's level.
  - Specials that recover to a ledge need the ground under their reach.
  - A projectile is dropped against Fox or Ness at level 5 or more.
  - Fully charged specials and grabs weigh 4, other attacks 1.
  - The last attack weighs a quarter. Moves passed over gain 0.2 per miss
    through eleven counters.
  - A weighted random pick starts the script. A fourth repeat in a row
    jumps instead.
  - Below level 3 the CPU may hold back at low damage.

## Implementation

- **Tables.** `tools/gen-computer-scripts.py` now also emits the attack
  lists as `ATTACKS`, a ground and an air slice per fighter in `nFTKind`
  order, split at the `-1` rows.
- **`computer::attack`.** Ports all of the functions above except the
  item paths. `Computer` gains:
  - the trait and the base objective;
  - the follow pacing and the wait-target fields;
  - the walk bursts and the idle wander state;
  - the attack counters and the repeat state;
  - `is_opponent_ra`.
- **`computer::behave`.** `process` now runs the trait each idle frame,
  sends every non-Training behaviour to `proc_default`, and follows the
  four new objectives. The counter-attack answers `is_opponent_ra` with
  the reflector script. `Opponent` now reports the target's kind, its
  hurtbox extent, its terminal velocity and its gravity. `World` gains
  `weapon_threats`.
- **Host tests.** Ten new tests cover:
  - the tables;
  - the first wait and the trait's picks;
  - the Attack and base objectives;
  - attack picks in reach and none out of reach or behind;
  - the fourth-repeat jump;
  - the shield release;
  - weapon prediction;
  - a full loop in which a level-3 CPU presses A or B.
- **`psp-game`.**
  - The VS CPU runs trait Default and behaviour Default at level 3. The
    Training dummy keeps Stand with trait None.
  - The capture override for a scene's CPU behaviour now clears the trait
    and carries into sudden death.
  - `vstimeup` pins its CPU to Stand, because its tie needs a CPU that
    lands no hit.
  - The new `vscpu` scene captures a VS battle at tick 690.
  - The capture log line now includes `player_damage`.

## Deviations

- **Items.** `ftComputerCheckFindItem`, TrackItem, UseItem and the item
  checks in the objectives and the attack chooser are not ported. They
  never apply while no items are in play.
- **Weapon view.** `psp-game` reports no weapons to the CPU yet. The
  prediction is tested on the host only.
- **Stand-ins.** The map body's width and top stand in for
  `damage_coll_size`. A moving floor stands in for Peach's Castle's
  `mpCollisionCheckExistPlatformLineID`.
- **Traits.** Only Default and None exist. The 1P and team traits and the
  Rush objective come with their modes.
