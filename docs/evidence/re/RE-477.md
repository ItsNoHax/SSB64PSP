# RE-477 — CRT overscan crop: what the N64 draws in the 10-pixel strip

Status: IMPLEMENTED (decomp source; N64 captures of the unmodified US ROM: the opening, the title, the mode select and the 1P menu; a temporary port probe of every 2D draw over all 173 game capture scenes; golden matrix rebaselined, captured three times identical, checked against the old goldens under the expected transform; PPSSPP profile A/B on seven scenes; How to Play and auto demo traces A/B; physical PSP pending: the PSP was disconnected)
Topics: rendering, projection, viewport, ui, camera, visual-regression, performance
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-engine/src/coord.rs`, `psp-runtime/src/{gu,meshdraw,particles,scene}.rs`, `psp-game/src/{main,demo_screen,results_screen,title_opening,campaign}.rs`, `refs/ssb-decomp-re/src/sys/objdisplay.c`, `refs/ssb-decomp-re/src/gm/gmcamera.c`

## Question

The user chose to emulate CRT overscan ([D-047](../../decisions/D-047.md),
superseding D-008). The visible box (10, 10)–(310, 230) of the N64's
320×240 frame now fills the PSP's 272 lines, and the strip around it is
cropped. Does the N64 draw anything in that strip that a player would
miss, such as the HUD, damage, timers or menus?

## Findings

### 1. The N64 never draws in the strip

- libgc clamps every camera's RDP scissor to 10 pixels inside the frame
  (`func_80016338` and its siblings in `sys/objdisplay.c`;
  `dGCCameraScissorTop/Bottom/Left/Right` = 10). The only setter,
  `gcSetCameraScissor`, has no callers. Sprite cameras
  (`lbCommonDrawSprite`) and 3D cameras both go through these display
  functions.
- Every camera viewport in the port's tables lies inside the box: battle,
  menus, selects, results, stage select, the opening's split viewports
  (10..90/90..230 and 10..150/150..230), How to Play's window (10..160 to
  230) and the staff roll's (20, 20)–(620, 460) on its 640×480 screen.
- N64 captures (Mupen64Plus, scratch config, ROM SHA-1 `e2929e10…`
  unchanged): the opening's room, the title with and without PRESS START,
  the mode select and the 1P mode menu. Each frame's non-black content
  spans x 9.7–310.3 and y 9.8–230.2 exactly, and the strip is black. Runs
  past frame 2,300 timed out in the driver, so no battle was captured. The
  scissor clamp above applies to battles as well.

On a CRT the strip held only black. Nothing important is lost, and RE-477
has no element in the strip to report.

### 2. What the old port drew there

A temporary probe (removed) logged every distinct sprite, fill, particle
and depth-mask rectangle that reached outside the box. It ran over all
173 game capture scenes. Under D-008 the port's scissor was the whole
pillarbox, so it drew the following in the strip, where the N64 clips
them:

- Backgrounds: the battle wallpapers (also in the strip around the
  stage), the title's full-frame layers, the menu stone tiles and select
  shutters that slide in from x −35 and x 310, the opening's sliding
  pictures and the black full-frame fills.
- Sprites crossing an edge: the 1P intro's sliding labels and figures
  (y down to −56), the "GO!"/countdown zoom (y −27), item arrows and tags
  near the top, Bonus records at x 330 (`bonusselect`), Yoshi's Island
  sprite 2737, How to Play's window frame (y to 234), the staff roll's
  crosshair at (0, 0) as it drops in, and the VS select's panel edge at x 8.
- Particles: Pokémon, the KO blast and the results' confetti crossing the
  edges.

With the crop each of these is clipped where the N64 clips it.

### 3. The battle camera's framing aspect

`FighterScene::tick_camera` framed the full battle viewport with the old
pillarbox's 362 / 272 (1.331). `gmCameraUpdateInterests` uses
`gGMCameraStruct.viewport_width / viewport_height`, which is 300 / 220
(1.364), and the PSP now shows exactly that aspect. The port now uses the
viewport's own aspect, so battles frame about 2.4% closer horizontally
than before, as on the N64. How to Play's 300×150 viewport already used
its own aspect and is unchanged.

## Change

- `ssb_engine::coord` is the only screen mapping. PSP x = 240 + (x − 160)
  · 272/220 and PSP y = 136 + (y − 120) · 272/220. The visible area is
  (55, 0)–(425, 272), 370 pixels whose centres lie inside the box. The
  module also provides rectangle, scissor and pixel-sampling helpers.
  `pillarboxed_viewport` is gone.
- `Gpu::set_viewport_n64` sends the viewport scale and centre as floats
  and clips the scissor to the visible area. `set_viewport_pillarboxed`
  is the whole N64 frame, cropped. `begin_frame` restores the last
  viewport and scissor after its full-screen clear. The calls that
  restored a full-screen scissor after N64 content now restore the
  cropped one. Only the no-pack stage select and a blocked campaign scene
  keep `set_viewport_fullscreen`.
- Callers routed through the mapping: sprites (plain, tiled, mirrored,
  glow), fills and fill triangles, the depth mask, 3D and screen
  particles, the wallpaper (one path now: `draw_wallpaper_n64` is gone),
  the pause border, the screen flash, the frozen picture, the transition
  and campaign wallpaper photo copies, How to Play's rectangles, the
  results' and title's particle cameras, and the no-pack select slots
  (formerly a hard-coded `59 + x · 17/15`). The asset viewer's
  projection aspect is the N64 frame's 4:3.

## Verification

- Golden matrix: 197 of 198 goldens changed, all rebaselined in one
  update. The rebaseline captured twice with no difference, and a third
  capture (the earlier verify run) matched byte for byte. After the
  rebaseline, `verify` passes 198 of 198. The unchanged row,
  `r2-mario-entry-pipe`, is a single-colour background with a particle
  quad drawn through its own fixed framing.
- Transform check: each old golden was resampled by the expected
  transform (old pillarbox x = 59 + n·272/240 at
  n = 160 + (X − 240)·220/272) and compared with the new capture inside
  the visible area, allowing one pixel of resampling misregistration.
  Pixels off by more than 64 levels (median per golden):
  - Asset viewer: 0.03% (max 1.8%).
  - Training, menus and results: 1.0% (max 3%).
  - VS battles, before the framing change: max 3.4%.
  - VS battles after it: up to 6%, from section 3's closer camera.
  
  Every bar column outside 55..424 is one colour in every golden.
  Side-by-side review of `f1-vs-team` shows only resampling edges.
- How to Play's `rng_trace` capture (`explain@4460`): 13,347 lines
  identical to `e5b3b07`'s, so it still matches the N64 (RE-472/474).
  The auto demo's two trace lines are identical too.
- PPSSPP profile (`capture_scene_file,profile,headless_capture`), average
  CPU per frame, `e5b3b07` → this change:
  - `vs4@4000`: 5.23 → 5.25 ms.
  - `explain@4100`: 2.62 → 2.63 ms.
  - `autodemo`: 4.49 → 4.49 ms.
  - `title@6000`: 2.27 → 2.27 ms.
  - `op-jungle@1200`: 2.70 → 2.71 ms.

  - `vsplayers@4000`: 1.74 → 1.74 ms.
  
  `vs4@4000` already ran at 5.2 ms at `e5b3b07`, above RE-471's 4.5.
  This change does not cause it. GE fill is larger: the battle viewport
