# RE-425 — Headgear accessories, model-part swaps and the entry vehicles

Status: IMPLEMENTED (decomp source, ROM records, packed-asset ROM tests, host tests, N64 warp-boot captures with per-frame entry counters and RDRAM display-list walks, PPSSPPHeadless goldens captured twice and broken by disabling each draw; no physical PSP)
Topics: fighter, costume, asset-pack, effect, animation, rendering, depth, draw-order, visual-regression
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-game/src/{modelpart,appear,appear_tests,kirby,kirby_copy,capture_kirby,grab,status,colanim,colanim_scripts,fighter}.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/motion/mod.rs`, `tools/gen-colanim-scripts.py`, `crates/ssb-rom/src/{fighter,pack,effect,scene_deps,skeleton}.rs`, `crates/ssb-rom/tests/model_parts.rs`, `tools/romtool/src/main.rs`, `psp-runtime/src/{meshdraw,scene}.rs`, `psp-game/src/{main,capture,players_screen,results_screen}.rs`, `crates/ssb-capture/src/lib.rs`, `tests/golden/*.png`

## Questions

1. `FTAttributes.accesspart` (Pikachu's hat, Jigglypuff's bow) was neither
   packed nor drawn. Where does it attach, in which costumes, and with what
   colours and state?
2. Motion-script model-part swaps (`ftParamSetModelPartID`) were packed for
   Kirby's copy hats only and never driven. Which parts do the scripts and
   the code set, on which joints?
3. Fox's Arwing, Captain Falcon's car and the Poké Ball (with its rays)
   were not drawn at the entry (RE-403). How does each move, sort and end?

## Source

- **Accessory** (`ftmanager.c:780-800`, `ftparam.c:1036-1054`,
  `ftdisplaymain.c:724-830`). `FTAccessPart` is `{joint_id, dl, mobjsubs,
  costume_matanim_joints}` at `FTAttributes` + 0x32C. For every costume but
  0 the joint gets a parts `GObj` whose `DObj` holds the list, its `MObj`s
  coloured once at the costume's frame (`lbCommonAddMObjForFighterPartsDObj`,
  then `gcRemoveAObjFromMObj`). `ftDisplayMainDrawAccessory` draws it in the
  joint's matrix when the joint has a list: Jigglypuff's before the joint's
  own list, Pikachu's after it. Pikachu: joint 11, 341 + 0x63F0; Jigglypuff:
  joint 6, 330 + 0x4A60 (`233_PurinMain.c:91`, `243_PikachuMain.c:117`).
  Every fighter the selects and results make runs the same code.
- **Model parts** (`ftparam.c:748-970`, `ftmain.c:574-596`, `ftmain.c:4437`).
  Each joint from 4 has `modelpart_id_base`/`_curr`, both 0 at the make for
  a joint whose high-detail descriptor has a list and -1 otherwise, and only
  for joints `setup_parts` creates. `SetModelPartID` (a 7-bit joint, a
  19-bit part; -2 is `joint_itemlight_id`) sets `curr`, `ResetModelPartAll`
  restores the bases, `HideModelPartAll` hides every joint, and
  `ftMainSetStatus` resets unless `FTSTATUS_PRESERVE_MODELPART`. Code
  setters: Link's make (joint 21 base -1, 19 base 0: the shield on his
  back), `ftCommonCatchWaitSetStatus` (Link's shield to his hand, Yoshi's
  joint 7 part 1), Kirby's copy and its loss (joint 6).
- **Never-made joints.** Samus's `setup_parts` (0xFFF803FF) skips
  descriptors 13-21, and Yoshi's descriptor 5; Samus's 20 and 21 and
  Yoshi's 5 have lists the port drew at rest. Samus's scripted parts on
  joints 17-25 (`dSamusMainMotion_0x0D10`) therefore do nothing.
- **Entry vehicles** (`efmanager.c:1282, 1495, 1555, 5211-5290, 5595-5740`,
  `ftcommonentry.c:88-106, 224-266, 321-330`, `reloc_data.us.h`):
  - Arwing: flags `0x4|0x1` (an outer `DObj` at the entry position, the
    tree from 161 + 0x2C30 under it), link 10, file 346's
    `llFoxSpecial2EntryArwingRAnimJoint` (0x9E0) or `...L...` (0x590) from
    the tree's root, a second kind-44 matrix on node 10. Each tick plays,
    ejects when node 0's script ends (120 frames) and `efManagerSortZNeg`s
    node 0: link 2 behind z -1000, else 20.
  - Car: flags `0x4|0x2`, 350 + 0x5FC0, `llCaptainSpecial2_6200_AnimJoint`
    on the tree, 0x6518 on nodes 3/5/7/9 (each also given a kind-44 matrix)
    and 0x6598 on 4/6/8/10; the root at the entry position, turned 180°
    for a leftward entry; ends with node 1's script; sorts node 1 with
    `SortZNeg`, or `SortZPos` (link 2 past z 1000) once turned. Falcon
    himself draws on link 1 on a leftward entry until his TopN is nearer
    than z -1000.
  - Poké Ball: 86 + 0x9430, `llITCommonDataMBallThrownR/LAnimJoint` (0x9690,
    0x95E0) and `...MatAnimJoint` (0x9810, 0x9740, whose node-3 scripts are
    byte-identical); link 20 at the make after `SortZNeg`, then each tick
    link 20 past z 1000 else 10 from the pose before the play; ends with the
    object. Its rays (85 + 0x628) are made at the entry position on motion
    flag 1, Appear frame 40, and last 50 frames.
  - Luigi's pipe is Mario's (`dFTLuigiData` names `llMarioSpecial2FileID`,
    `ftdata.c:2881`); Kirby's rightward star plays 348 + 0x1EA0.

## Evidence

- **ROM tests** (`crates/ssb-rom/tests/model_parts.rs`): the joint table in
  `ssb_game::modelpart` equals every fighter's `setup_parts` and its
  descriptors' lists; only Pikachu and Jigglypuff have an accessory, at the
  offsets above; costumes 1-3 pack it on the joint's node from its list and
  no other node or costume 0 has one; every part the scripts or code can set
  on a made joint is packed from its `FTModelPart` list or is exactly the
  node's own mesh (at least 30 packed); the entry slots name their trees'
  nodes and the car's four sprites are billboards.
- **Script walk**: `ssb_game::motion::model_part_events` finds the
  decompilation's `SetModelPartID` events per fighter (a host test pins
  them: Fox (17, 0), Donkey Kong seven, Samus eleven, Link twelve, Yoshi
  two, Captain Falcon five, Kirby nine, Ness five, none for Mario, Luigi,
  Pikachu or Jigglypuff).
- **N64 warp-boot builds** (`scmanager.c` boots Training or a two-player VS
  battle from a per-build fighter/costume table, `ifcommon.c` fixes the
  entry focus id to the port's, `ftcommonentry.c` counts each player's
  Appear updates at 0x80780020; reverted afterwards). The port's capture
  RNG gives focus id 1 (fighters enter at ticks 204 and 219); the N64 build
  with id 1 enters them at frames 196 and 211, so port tick t is N64 frame
  t - 8.
  - Training, Pikachu costume 1 against Jigglypuff costume 2 and Jigglypuff
    3 against Pikachu 3: the red and green party hats and the blue and green
    bows sit and colour as the port draws them. The accessory's vertex
    loads (341 + 0x6360 ×4, + 0x63A0 ×5; 330 + 0x49E0 ×4, + 0x4A20 ×4) are
    lit on the N64 (RDRAM walk, frame 300) and in `romtool lighting`, in the
    same place of the joint's walk.
  - Fox against Captain Falcon, frames 150-420 every 2: the Arwing and the
    car match the port's frames in place, size and pose at every compared
    tick (234, 244, 249, 254, 264, 274, 279, 294, 309). An RDRAM walk of
    frame 256 finds both bodies under `G_RM_AA_ZB_OPA_SURF` with
    `G_ZBUFFER` (the fighters' state left on the head) and their head-1
    glows under `G_RM_AA_ZB_XLU_SURF`; the port had drawn the bodies with no
    depth test, so the car's far side and the Arwing's exhaust glow showed
    through. Draw order in that frame: Fox and Falcon (link 9), then the car
    and the Arwing (link 20).
  - Pikachu against Jigglypuff: the ball's flight, spin textures, opening at
    frame 40 and the rays (and their spread and fade) match at ticks 214,
    218, 230, 236, 254 and 284 (224: see the deviations).
  - Training with B (Fox, Donkey Kong, Ness, Samus) and down+B (Link) at
    frame 300: Fox holds his Blaster and Link's shield is off his back,
    as the port now draws them. The N64 camera frames these closer than the
    port's scenes, so the Donkey Kong, Ness and Samus parts were checked
    against the script events, not pixel by pixel.
- **Selects and results.** A temporary build (reverted) put Pikachu in
  costume 1 and Jigglypuff in costume 2 on the results screen: both wear
  their accessory. Both character selects draw through the same
  `players_screen::draw_fighters` path.

## Implementation

- **`ssb-game`.** `modelpart::ModelParts` (`present`, `base`, `curr`,
  `is_modify`) with `set`, `set_default`, `reset_all`, `hide_all` and
  `node_part` (`ABSENT` for a never-made joint, `HIDDEN` for -1), made in
  `Fighter::new` with the make's Kirby and Link defaults; the motion
  interpreter runs opcodes 40-42; `set_any_status_preserve` resets through
  the generated `PRESERVE_MODELPART` table (68 calls,
  `tools/gen-colanim-scripts.py`); Link's and Yoshi's catch-wait parts and
  Kirby's copy, loss and inhale escape set joint 6's base. `modelpart::demo`
  gives the selects' and results' fighters their make-time parts.
  `appear::Entry` gains `rays_ticks` (started on flag 1 for Pikachu and
  Jigglypuff) and `is_link_1` (Falcon's leftward entry).
- **Pack v77**, 34,880,272 bytes (+291,024), SHA-256
  `b6d6b19d183d875579821b61aa34f59231c0096c2afd1316577d1ec10e4020ef`; no
  layout change.
  - 135 model-part meshes: every reachable part, converted in the whole
    graph's walk (a node without a list gets the part's list inserted at its
    place) or, from another file (Fox's Blaster, 315 + 0x3F0), on its own
    under the fighter seed; keyed `modelpart_costume(part, costume)` and
    stored only where it differs from the lookup's fallback. Kirby's copy
    hats are part of this set.
  - 6 accessory meshes (`accessory_costume(costume)`, 0x2000 + costume):
    converted in the costume's walk before or after the joint's list, with
    any later node whose state it changed given its own override. The
    costume-override comparison now matches plan slots rather than node
    indices.
  - Entry slots from 0x110 (`ENTRY_ANIMS`): the Arwing's two tables (file
    346's scripts on file 161's nodes), the car's table and wheel scripts,
    the ball's rightward table and Kirby's rightward star; the ball's
    material table is 0x9740, no longer 0x950C; the car's nodes 3/5/7/9 are
    flagged billboards; the Arwing's and the car's trees convert under the
    fighter seed (depth test and write, opaque).
- **`psp-runtime`.** `meshdraw::Look` (costume key, per-descriptor parts,
  accessory order) and `draw_fighter_posed`; a node draws its part's mesh
  (this costume, then the part's costume 0, then its own), nothing when
  hidden or never made, and its accessory beside it in a plain costume;
  `draw_object_posed_scaled_hiding`. `scene::ENTRY_PARTS` describes each
  entry part (key, animation slot, root placement, left turn, end node,
  link rule, clock, extra kind-44 nodes).
- **`psp-game`.** Fighters draw with their parts and accessory in battle, on
  both selects and on the results screen. `sync_entry` sorts each part onto
  its link as its update does; the entries draw on links 2 (with a
  leftward Falcon on link 1, before layer 0), 10 and 20 (after links
  16-18). A capture line `scene@tick` overrides the capture tick for N64
  matching.

## Verification

- **Goldens.** Seven added, identical twice: `f1-training-pikachu-hat`,
  `f1-training-purin-bow`, `f1-training-fox-blaster` (the `fox` scene),
  `f1-vs-arwing`, `f1-vs-car`, `f1-vs-ball`, `f1-vs-rays`. Eight
  rebaselined, identical twice: `f1-training-samus` (76 pixels at 2×),
  `-samus-shot` (52) and `-samus-bomb` (136): descriptors 20-21 on the arm
  cannon no longer draw; `f1-training-donkey` (1,040): the Giant Punch
  charge's face and hands; `f1-training-ness` (100) and `-ness-thunder`
  (76): open hands; `f1-training-link-bomb` (324): the shield moves to his
  hand; `f1-vs-team` (908): Donkey Kong's open hand.
- **Negative tests.** A build that drops the accessory, the parts and the
  new entry parts changes all fourteen affected goldens: `pikachu-hat` 920,
  `purin-bow` 1,028, `fox-blaster` 92, `vs-arwing` 12,192 (the Arwing and
  the car at the left edge), `vs-ball` 2,712, `vs-rays` 31,180 and the
  eight rebaselined ones by their counts above.
- **Host tests.** The part state, a Donkey Kong dash's face and its reset, a
  preserving switch, Link's shield default, the script walk, the rays on
  frame 40 and Falcon's link. Breaking the preserve lookup fails
  `a_preserving_switch_keeps_the_part`.
- **Gates.** Workspace tests with an absolute `SSB64_ROM` and one thread
  (1,574 passed), pinned 1.98.0 Clippy with warnings denied, rustfmt,
  `ssb-game` and `ssb-rom` as `no_std`, and both PSP release builds (the
  viewer's six existing warnings). The release `run` is 21,764 bytes (was
  20,480; frame 18,544); the largest frame is `enter_training` at 159,552
  bytes (the fighter's part state), then `EffectVisuals::sync_ko` at
  95,368.

## Deviations and gaps

- **Detail.** The port draws high detail only; VS with three or four
  fighters uses low detail on the N64 (`scvsbattle.c:188`), including the
  parts' low-detail records.
- **Texture parts** (`ftParamSetTexturePartID`, the eyes and mouths, 369
  script events) are not ported; the faces keep texture 0. This is likely
  the RE-420 Kirby Win mouth difference.
- **Demo scripts.** The selects' and results' fighters keep their make-time
  parts; their demo statuses' script events are not run.
- **Model-part state leak.** A part's conversion changes only its own node;
  a later node whose inherited state the part's list changes keeps its own
  mesh. The accessory is converted with that leak.
- **Main material scripts** of `FTModelPart`s (Samus's joints 17-22, never
  made) are not packed.
- **Arwing cockpit.** At port tick 264 (N64 frame 256) the N64 shows Fox's
  head above the Arwing's hull for about one frame; the port hides it
  behind the hull. Not resolved.
- **Ball near the camera.** At port tick 224 the N64 shows the ball's edge
  at the right of the screen; the port shows none (probably near-plane
  clipping). Not investigated.
- **Entry camera.** The port's entry focus zoom (id 2) was not compared;
  every comparison used id 1.
- **Luigi's pipe and Kirby's rightward star** now draw (the pipe's key and
  the star's packed rightward table) but were not compared with N64
  captures or given goldens.

## Conclusion

Pikachu's hat and Jigglypuff's bow draw on their joints in every costume
but 0, in their costume colours and lighting, in battle, on the selects and
on the results screen. Every fighter's motion scripts and code now swap
model parts, which fixes hands, faces, Link's shield and Fox's Blaster and
hides the descriptors Samus and Yoshi never make. Fox's Arwing, Captain
Falcon's car and the Poké Ball and its rays play their own tables, sort
onto links 2, 10 and 20 as their updates do, and match N64 captures frame
for frame; Luigi's pipe and Kirby's rightward star draw as well.
