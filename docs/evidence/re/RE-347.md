# RE-347 — Ness, PK Thunder ownership and signed motion angles

Status: COMPLETE (bounded host port, decomp/ROM checks, PSP builds and static PPSSPP smoke; shared map, item hurt and physical PSP remain)
Topics: gameplay, fighter, status, hit-resolution, weapon, animation, asset-pipeline
Relevant files: `crates/ssb-game/src/ness.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/ness_attack.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/ness_weapon.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/ness_tests.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/weapon.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/kirby_copy.rs`, `crates/ssb-game/src/grab.rs`, `tools/extract_ness_moves.py`, `tools/extract_purin_moves.py`, `tools/gen-anim-table.py`

**Question.** How do Ness's fighter, weapon and item callbacks share state,
what happens to PK Thunder on reflection/allocation failure, and how are
motion-script angles above 361 interpreted?

**Sources.** US `238_NessMainMotion.c`, `239_NessMain.c`,
`240_NessSpecial1.c`, `228_KirbyMainMotion.c`; `ftnessspecial{n,hi,lw}.c`,
`ftnessstatus.h`, `ftkirbycopynessspecialn.c`, `ftcommonjumpaerial.c`,
`ftcommonattacklw3.c`, `ftcommonattackair.c`, `ftmain.c`, `ftphysics.c`,
`wpnesspkfire.c`, `wpnesspkthunder.c`, `wpmain.c`, `itnesspkfire.c`,
`itmap.c`, `itprocess.c`, `itmanager.c`, and the figatrees named by
`dFTNessMotionDescs` and `dFTKirbyMotionDescs`.

**Findings.**

- **Normals and throws.** Jab gates are 10/8, with a third jab; down tilt
  sets its repeat gate at frame 11. Forward/back throws release at 27,
  deal 16 and enter `DamageFlyN`; their joint-30 boxes deal 10 to
  bystanders while excluding the held victim. The source has one forward
  smash variant for Ness and Jigglypuff, correcting Jigglypuff's routing.
- **Double jump.** The common Ness physics callback takes XYZ from TransN,
  keeps input velocity X separately, clamps/drifts it, then adds the root
  motion's horizontal drift. It has no gravity or fast-fall check during
  the 75-frame figatree. Special entry/switch flags clear fast fall.
- **PK Fire.** Accessory flag 0 at frame 20 reads the current situation.
  Ground/air angles are −3.6/−38 degrees, speeds 73/95, and lifetime 20.
  Ness uses TopN plus {100·lr,180,0}; Kirby uses {240·lr,190,0} and its
  own 72/60-frame clips and copied stale ID. A fighter hit makes an
  unpickable flame item 160 units along the normalized velocity; a map
  hit destroys the spark without a flame. The source item manager has a
  separate 16-entry allocation table. The port preserves that boundary,
  including allocation failure without retries. Initial Fall resets XY
  velocity; later frames use gravity 0.45, terminal speed 55, floor wait
  with zero velocity and wall/ceiling rebound ×0.2. Life starts at 100,
  scale is `0.5 + life/200` before decrement, and fighter rehit is 16
  ticks, including the initial status-selection tick.
- **PK Thunder.** Startup is 24 frames, launch delay 30 hold frames,
  gravity delay 25 and lost-head end delay 30. The head lasts 160 ticks,
  moves at 60 and turns toward a stick with `|x|+|y| > 45`: six degrees
  when the difference is at least 45 degrees, otherwise difference/7.5.
  Four separately allocated trails spawn in three-tick steps, sample the
  twelve signed-16-bit position entries and share trail hit records.
  Head damage is 6; trail damage is 3. Owner status/motion identity and
  self-contact destroy the head and its trails.
- **Self-launch.** After the launch gate, a rectangle of half-width 250
  and half-height 370 centred 150 above Ness triggers the 200-speed blast.
  It lasts 28 updates and decelerates by 43/7 along its angle. Passable
  floors are skipped for the first three frames; ground/air switches keep
  hit records and skip frame-zero script initialization. Grounded angles
  above 155 degrees and steep landing impacts enter shared DownBounce.
  Recovery retains the up-special gravity callback (0.5), then aerial
  recovery enters helpless fall with drift 0.6 and lag 0.17.
- **Reflection.** First reflection allocates a replacement head before
  deleting the old one; a full weapon table consumes the old head without
  a replacement. Direction is normalized from reflector position plus
  {0,250,0}, with Z velocity explicitly zero. Later reflections reset life
  and apply the ordinary X-facing turn. Reflected trails retain the source
  cumulative parent-rotation-minus-90-degree collision offsets.
  **Explicit deviation:** repeated reflection retires the old trail group
  before creating a new group. The decomp documents its dangling-head
  `GObj` crash: resetting head lifetime loses old trail references, which
  later access an ejected head. The Rust port preserves lifetime reset but
  prevents that invalid ownership state.
- **Magnet and bat.** Magnet starts in 15 frames, has a four-tick gravity
  delay then gravity 0.8, a minimum 30 hold ticks and sticky B release.
  Absorb is active during hold/hit, heals twice staled projectile damage,
  faces the projectile and returns from the hit clip at frame 14 without
  resetting the hold timers. Its US TopN special collision uses offset
  {300,195,0}, radius 430. Eligible Fireball, Blaster, Charge Shot, Thunder
  Jolt and PK heads/sparks enter that path; PK trails are not absorbable.
  Bat reflection is frames 16..22, TopN offset {0,150,0}, radius 300.
- **Script transcription corrections.** The ten-bit angle is signed:
  Ness down air 934 becomes −90; Fox drill 954 becomes −70. The extractor
  now decodes that field and coalesces same-frame damage/size edits into
  one window, retaining the existing hit generation. Twenty Ness moves
  contain 44 authored collision windows. The US blast's whole-body
  invincibility is frames 0..10 and throws use 3..27.
- **Animation.** Forty-one slots append after `FuraSleep`, keeping all
  previous ordinals: nineteen Ness common clips, twenty own clips and two
  Kirby PK Fire clips. Ness also receives existing grab/thrown/sleep slots.
  The `(fighter, slot)` pack lookup permits retaining pack version 38.

**Verification.** 1,077 workspace tests with `SSB64_ROM`; Rust 1.98 host
clippy (`--workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings`) and rustfmt clean.
Eighteen Ness-focused tests cover routing, accessory timing, copied offsets,
jab/repeat gates, signed knockback, double-jump drift/fast-fall, launch and
recovery clocks, owner loss, allocation failures, steering and trail cleanup,
reflection, Magnet staling/heal and switches, bat timing, flame allocation,
shrink/rehit/lifetime, throw release and bystander exclusion, and preserved
blast hit records. Both PSP builds pass, including `psp-game` with
`strict_render`. `romtool anims --verify` agrees on 789 lengths; regenerating
the table reproduces it byte for byte. Pack v38 is 23,659,808 bytes; SHA-256
`0353ac343aa59236e88317b36539f1152902567fcf0dc6cd9e7503e7809c8f4b`.
ROM-derived output is not committed. PPSSPPHeadless `r2-ness-fighter`
matches its golden with zero differing pixels.

**Limits.** The static smoke proves pack/render integration, not playable
Ness. Ness and copied PK Fire are host-only; their weapons, effects, bat
parts and PK Thunder model pitch are not drawn in the game. Fighter map
collision remains floor-only: wall sliding/rebounds, grounded edge stops
and cliff catches remain deferred. `DownBounceD` has no update/clip, so a
downward self-launch or steep impact reaches that ordinal without recovery.
PK Fire incoming item damage (life minus three times highest queued damage),
moving-group attachment, general items, per-part hurtboxes/intangibility,
shield accumulation, fire/electric elemental reactions, weapon set-off/hop,
sound and RNG-driven visual texture
choices remain shared-system gaps. Whole-body invincibility uses the existing
non-registering hit approximation. No full golden matrix or physical-PSP
validation was run; these findings do not close `P2`.
