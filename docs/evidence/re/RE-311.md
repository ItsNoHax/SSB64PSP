# RE-311 — Real-normal texgen coverage for 3-point compensation

Status: COMPLETE (host and PPSSPP smoke; physical PSP not captured)
Topics: texture, texgen, asset-pipeline, pack, psp-ge

Relevant files: `tools/romtool/src/filter_coverage.rs`, `tools/romtool/src/main.rs`

**Problem.** `primitive_filter_coverage()` returned empty coverage for every
texgen primitive. The compensator then trained on the dense full-image
lattice (S10.5 step 8, phase 0) and validated on the phase-4 lattice. This
choice is safe, but most of that lattice is unreachable for a small
`gSPTexture` scale on a large tile. For example, `0x0400/0x0200` on the
Metal stage's 159x79 tile generates 16x8 texels. The lattice also does not
match the sample distribution that real normals produce.

**Source chain.** The runtime chain is fixed and already verified (RE-226 to
RE-229, D-038, D-040). The camera look-at is quantised to signed bytes. It
is carried into object space as `M^T * l` and normalised
(`quantize_lookat_basis`, `transform_lookat_basis`). The dot product is the
raw `i8` normal divided by 127. The curve is the ordinary `(d+1)/4` or the
linear `acos(-d)/2pi`. Then come the `gSPTexture` scale, the tile origin on
clamped axes only, and S10.5 truncation. The coverage uses
`regular_texgen_uv` and `linear_texgen_uv` for this chain, so it has no
second copy of the arithmetic. At runtime, the ordinary texgen draw uses the
GE texture matrix. RE-228 proves that matrix against `regular_texgen_uv`.
The linear draw uses `linear_texgen_uv` on the CPU. Runtime rendering is
unchanged.

**Camera states are not statically enumerable.** `gmCameraLookAtFuncMatrix`
(`gmcamera.c`) calls `syMatrixLookAtReflectF` every frame with the live
`cobj->vec.eye/at/up`. The `objdisplay.c` look-at camera variants do the same
with the live CObj vectors. The fighter joint animation also rotates each
texgen node freely. Thus no finite set of real poses exists. A single frozen
camera would be an invented pose, so none is used.

**Pose-independent bound.** The RSP normalises each basis row, so for every
pose and node transform, Cauchy-Schwarz gives `|dot| <= |n|/127` for each
axis. Here `n` is the longest real normal of the primitive. This bound gives
an inclusive S10.5 box (`TexgenBound`), padded by one unit for f32 rounding.
The rows are *not* assumed to be orthogonal. A non-uniform node scale makes
them non-orthogonal after normalisation. That case can reach the box
corners, which an orthonormal pose (a disk) never reaches. Every generated
vertex UV, and therefore every interpolated fragment UV, is inside the box.

**Coverage.**

* Poses: the 60 rotations of the icosahedral group, each applied to 6
  integer base quaternions. This gives 360 training poses. A disjoint set of
  6 other bases gives 360 holdout poses. The icosahedral group is the most
  uniform finite subgroup of SO(3), so the pose set is a quadrature over all
  orientations and not a guessed view. Only `+ - * /` and `sqrt` are used,
  so the ordinary-texgen coverage is bit-identical on every host.
* Broad points: for each pose, every triangle's three real normals are sent
  through the exact generator. The generated UV triangle contributes its
  vertices and its N=8 barycentric lattice. In addition, `reach_points`
  samples the full disk that one vertex can reach under orthonormal poses:
  48 rings by 384 spokes, with rational circle parametrisation and no
  trigonometry. Without `reach_points`, 360 poses under-sampled the rim. On
  a synthetic octahedron, only 89% of random-pose vertex cells had a
  training point.
* Critical probes: every reached texel cell gets RE-310's `add_cell` probe
  pattern, clipped to the bound. The pattern includes the diagonal branch
  switch, the half texel, the GE 1/16 boundaries, and the seams. The union
  of hundreds of poses covers a reached cell much more densely than one
  authored triangle does. Clipping per triangle measured 8 to 17 s for one
  Metal Mario primitive. Clipping per cell measures 0.13 to 0.26 s.
* Budget and weighting: training and holdout each get
  `max(authored floor, 16 x reachable texels)` points. This is the old
  lattice density over the reachable box only. Critical probes can fill at
  most 25% of the training budget, stratified by cell. Real-pose broad
  points fill the rest. The packer adds a full-box regularization of one
  point per reachable texel at a rotating sub-texel phase. That is 1/16 of
  the old training density. On the 48x42 body texture, the regularization is
  2,107 points, and the merged, deduplicated training set is 34,092 points
  (6%).
* Overfitting measurement: a fixed budget of 8,192 points on the 48x42
  Metal Mario body texture (2,016 texels, 4 free channels each) cut its own
  training SSE by 36%. The independent holdout moved by only -15% to +2%.
  After the budget was scaled to 16 points per texel, the holdout falls by
  22% to 25%. When the critical-probe share was 75%, the result was worse.
  Those probes sit at a small number of fixed sub-texel phases.
* Holdout: the disjoint poses, the reach grid staggered by half a step, and
  the holdout half of the probe pattern. The training points are removed.

**Admission.** A bounded texgen candidate must pass RE-310's holdout gate on
the real-pose holdout (SSE, `>=8`, `>=32`, max +8, visible SSE, and cutout).
It must *also* pass a separate conservative gate. That gate is the old
phase-4 lattice restricted to the bound, and some pose can generate every
point on it. Therefore, a real-coverage improvement cannot hide a
regression anywhere in the reachable region.

**Fallback to full-tile coverage.** The old full-tile path is used when the
primitive has a material animation, which can apply a live `MaterialUv`
affine on top of the generated coordinates. It is also used when the
primitive has no resolved texture or no triangles.

**Measurement mode.** `ROMTOOL_TEXGEN_COVERAGE=full-tile` gives the pre-RE-311
optimisation and gating. It rebuilt the exact RE-310 pack, with SHA-256
`5179c3b3...cb7cba`. The `full-tile-per-primitive` mode also converts each
primitive separately, so each real-coverage report line has a counterpart on
the same sample sets. Its pack is also byte-identical to the RE-310 pack,
because no full-tile texgen candidate was accepted. Every texgen conversion
prints a `texgen-filter-comp` line. The line gives the outcome and the
source and packed metrics on three sets: the real holdout, the conservative
box, and the old full-image lattice.

Under full-tile coverage, zero texgen conversions are accepted: 183 fail the
solver's own gate and 14 fail promotion. Under real coverage, 14 are
accepted. For each texture:

* Metal Mario body, file 300 `0x2490`, 48x42 CI8, 14 primitives. Full-tile
  coverage accepted 0 of 14. The continuous solve reached only 18% on the
  full lattice, and promotion requires 25%. Real coverage accepts 14 of 14.
  Summed real-holdout SSE falls from 4,939,494 to 3,620,372 (-26.7%). The
  conservative box falls from 3,341,352 to 2,646,484 (-20.8%). `>=32` stays
  at 0.
* Shared metal reflection map, file 302 `0x30`, 32x32/64x32/32x64 CI4. This
  covers 161 ordinary and 13 linear conversions for the polygon fighters and
  Metal Mario. The solver cannot reach its own 5% SSE, fewer-`>=8`, and max
  gate on either coverage, so the source texels are kept. This is a limit of
  the fixed-function filter for this high-contrast map (max error 81/255).
  It is not a coverage limit.
* Link's texgen texture, file 324 `0xB1B0`, 8x8, 8 conversions. On the real
  holdout, the candidate improves SSE by 17.5% and halves `>=32` (289 to
  154). The conservative box regresses: SSE +20%, `>=32` from 17 to 24. The
  box gate rejects the candidate. That region is reachable only by
  non-orthogonal bases, so keeping the source there is the correct result.
* MVCommon, file 52 `0x8EB0`: no solve on either coverage.

No packed result is worse than the full-tile result on any set. One
real-coverage conversion fewer than full-tile occurs, because two primitives
produced identical coverage and share one cache entry. The pack has 1,834
textures (+13) and is 29,627,552 bytes (+298,768). The growth comes from the
per-primitive variants of the Metal Mario body texture. Each primitive's
coverage differs, so each accepted variant is packed separately. A
per-texture union of coverage could merge them. That work is not done in
this batch.

**Verification.** Host tests cover these cases:

* The icosahedral group: 60 unit rotations, closed under composition.
* Known normals through the identity pose.
* Orthonormality of every pose.
* Ordinary texgen on an octahedron: bounded, disk-shaped, with disjoint
  training and holdout.
* A short normal gives a smaller bound.
* Linear texgen equals `linear_texgen_uv` exactly.
* The clamped-origin, small-scale bound of the real stage tile.
* 256 orthogonal and 256 non-orthogonal random bases outside the
  quadrature. All stay inside the bound, and at least 99% land in training
  cells.
* Lattice and regularization geometry.
* A pinned coverage hash.

A ROM-gated test runs Metal Mario (300), polygon Fox (303), and the Metal
stage's linear texgen (117) through `texgen_filter_coverage`. It checks
determinism, the bound, the span limits, and the same random-pose
containment and cell coverage with the real normals.
