# D-049: The N64 Audio System Is Ported Whole and Synthesized at Run Time on Its Own Thread

Category: Platform & Toolchain

**Decision:** The game's audio is the original's own: libultra's `n_audio`
synthesizer and compressed-sequence player, the FGM sound-effect engine in
`n_env.c`, and the `syAudio` layer of `src/sys/audio.c`, translated into
`ssb_engine::audio` as one portable subsystem. Nothing is converted ahead
of time except into the pack's layout.

- **Data.** `romtool pack` copies the ROM's audio files into one section at
  the end of the pack: `S1_music.sbk`, `B1_sounds1` and `B1_sounds2`
  (`.ctl` and `.tbl`, VADPCM kept compressed), `fgm.unk`, `fgm.tbl`,
  `fgm.ucd`, and the constant tables the synthesizer reads from the ROM
  (the resampler's filter, `n_eqpower`, the custom reverb's parameters and
  presets, `gSYSinTable`). About 4.4 MB. It is read once at boot into a
  buffer that is never freed and stays resident for the process's life.
  The repository holds only where these lie in the ROM
  ([D-048](D-048.md)).
- **Synthesis.** VADPCM is decoded at run time. The RSP microcode's
  commands (ADPCM, resample, envelope mixer, mixer, interleave, pole
  filter) are implemented directly in integer fixed point, without command
  lists. The synthesizer's timing runs at the N64's real output rate,
  32006 Hz (`osAiSetFrequency(32000)` on NTSC), in frames of 552 or 368
  samples chosen by `syAudioThreadMain`'s rule, each cut into 184-sample
  sub-frames.
- **Output.** On the PSP a dedicated audio thread, at a higher priority
  than the game's, renders frames and plays them through the 32 kHz SRC
  channel in fixed 512-sample blocks.
- **Calls.** Game code makes the decomp's calls where the decomp makes
  them (`ssb_game::sound`: `syAudioPlayBGM`, `func_800269C0_275C0`, ...).
  They reach the one `AudioSystem` synchronously, under a lock the audio
  thread also takes for each frame (`SharedAudio`), as the N64 game calls
  the FGM engine under `osSetIntMask`. A play returns its handle at once.
- **Media Engine.** Not used. It needs kernel-mode code that rust-psp does
  not bind, PPSSPP does not run it, its cache is not coherent with the
  Allegrex and it has no VFPU. The synthesizer's render path performs no
  system calls, allocation or trig, so it could move there later.

**Deviations from the N64:**

- The PSP plays the 32006 Hz stream at 32000 Hz: 0.02% slow, a third of a
  cent flat.
- The audio thread is paced by the PSP's output, not by the video retrace.
  A game call lands before the next frame the audio thread renders.
- Boot builds the final configuration (the custom reverb, a 72-entry FGM
  oscillator pool) directly. It does not restart the audio thread twice
  as `scManagerRunLoop` does, and the game does not spin on
  `syAudioGetSettingsUpdated` or `syAudioGetRestarting`.
- The game does not spin on `syAudioCheckBGMPlaying` after
  `syAudioStopBGMAll`. The BGM status machine handles a stop and a play in
  order.
- `__n_initFromBank` writes one channel state past its 16-entry array. The
  port gives it a 17th entry that nothing reads.
- The microcode is not decompiled. Its commands follow the reimplementation
  in BattleShip's mixer (MIT) and are checked against PCM recorded from
  the original under Mupen64Plus's HLE RSP.

**Reasoning:** Sounds in this game are programs, not samples. FGM scripts
spawn voices through articulation programs with their own oscillators and
random numbers. Sequences change programs, tempo and loops as they play.
Rendering them ahead of time would mean one recording per sound and per
interaction. Translating the engine keeps every sound, its timing and its
interaction with the game, as the gameplay port does. Compressed VADPCM is
4.4 MB, against about 17 MB decoded. The worst single sequence needs 74%
of the music bank, so per-sequence sample sets would save little. The
section's lifetime must exceed any scene's, because the audio thread reads
it at any time.

`crates/ssb-engine/src/audio/`, `crates/ssb-game/src/sound/`,
`psp-runtime/src/audio.rs`.
